Overview

What is first degree heart block?

First-degree heart block is a type of cardiac conduction abnormality where there is a prolonged delay in the conduction of electrical impulses from the atria → ventricles

What causes conduction blocks in the heart?

Damage ( fibrosis, calcification, necrosis ) to the conduction system ( AV node or His Purkinje system )

What are the ECG findings on patients with different types of conduction blocks in the heart?

  • First-degree conduction block - increased P wave length (P-R interval) due to slow signal conduction
  • Second-degree conduction block - increased P wave length, delayed/irregular QRS complexes
  • Third-degree conduction block - complete lack of QRS complex

Recall one treatment option for patients with conduction blocks.

  • Stopping drugs that cause conduction blocks of the AV tracts — i.e beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers
  • Pacemakers in severe cases

Describe the findings we would expect to see on a first-degree conduction block ECG.

  • Persistent prolongation of PR segment/interval caused by slower AV conduction
  • Regular rhythm : 1:1 ratio of P-waves to QRS complexes

Describe what causes a first-degree conduction block.

First degree heart block is often a progressive disease of ageing — It is the most benign heart block

What is second degree heart block?

Second-degree heart block is a type of atrioventricular (AV) block where some electrical impulses from the atria fail to conduct to the ventricles, leading to intermittent beats

Recall the two types of second degree heart block.

  • Mobitz I
  • Mobitz II

Describe the findings we would expect to see on a second-degree conduction block (Mobitz I) ECG.

  • Progressive prolongation of the PR interval until beat skipped — i.e prolongation continually gets longer
  • Most P-waves followed by QRS; but some P-waves are not
  • Regularly irregular
  • Also called Wenckebach

Describe what causes a second-degree conduction block.

Second degree heart block is often caused by a diseased AV node

Second-Degree Heart Block (Mobitz I).

Describe the difference between a Mobitz I and a Mobitz II conduction block.

  • A Mobitz I conduction block is characterised by the progressive prolongation of the P-R interval before the lack of QRS complex
  • A Mobitz II conduction block is characterised by the lack of QRS complex in a lot of heartbeats, however there is no progressive prolongation of P-R interval

Describe the findings we would expect to see on a second-degree conduction block (Mobitz II) ECG.

  • P-waves are regular, but only some are followed by QRS — i.e non-conducted P waves
  • No P-R prolongation
  • Regularly irregular : successes to failures (e.g. 2:1) or random

Can rapidly deteriorate into third degree heart block
