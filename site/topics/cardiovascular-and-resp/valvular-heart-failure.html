Overview

What is infective endocarditis?

Infective endocarditis is an infection of the endocardium or vascular endothelium of the heart. Typically affects the heart valves. — The infection is usually caused by bacteria, but it can also be caused by fungi or other microorganisms.

What usually causes infective endocarditis?

  • It is usually the result of bacteria entering the blood stream and forming ”a vegetation” (a bacterial infection surrounded by a layer of platelets and fibrin) in the endocardium
  • Streptococci (20-40 % of cases) are the most common infection

Which parts of the heart are more susceptible to infection in infective endocarditis?

Valves

How do you diagnose infective endocarditis?

Duke’s criteria

Recall the components of the major and minor criteria in Duke’s criteria. Major criteria

  • Persistently +ve blood culture for typical organisms
  • ECHO: vegetation, dehiscence (splitting) of prosthetic valve, abscess
  • New valvular regurgitation murmur
  • Coxiella burnetti infection (bacteria) Minor criteria
  • Predisposing heart condition or IV drug use
  • Fever greater than 38ºC
  • Vascular: emboli to organs, brain
  • Immunologic: glomerulonephritis, Oslers nodes, Roth spots
  • Positive blood cultures that do not meet specific criteria

Describe how the Duke’s criteria is used to diagnose infective endocarditis. Definite endocarditis

  • 2 major clinical criteria
  • 1 major and any 3 minor clinical criteria
  • 5 minor criteria
  • +ve gram stain or culture from surgery or autopsy Possible endocarditis
  • 1 major and ›1 minor clinical criteria
  • 3 minor criteria Rejected endocarditis
  • Resolution after less than 4 days antibiotic treatment
  • No evidence of infection after surgery
  • Definite or possible criteria not met

Describe three features commonly seen in the diagnosis of infective endocarditis.

  • Fever, malaise, sweats and unexplained weight loss are common symptoms
  • There may be a new heart murmur on examination
  • Blood tests show anaemia and raised markers of infection
  • Blood cultures may isolate a microorganism
  • Echocardiogram can show a vegetation, abscess, valve perforation and/or new dehiscence of prosthetic valve — Often there is regurgitation of the affected valve — Transoesophageal echo has higher sensitivity compared with transthoracic

What is malaise?

Malaise is a general feeling of discomfort, illness, or lack of well- being

What is cardiac decompensation?

This is the inability of the heart to maintain adequate circulation

Recall three clinical features of cardiac decompensation in infective endocarditis.

  • Shortness of breath
  • Frequent coughing
  • Swelling of the legs and abdomen
  • Fatigue
  • Raised JVP
  • Lung crackles
  • Oedema

Recall two other clinical features of cardiac decompensation in infective endocarditis.

  • Vascular and embolic phenomena — (stroke, Janeway lesions, splinter/ conjunctival haemorrhages)
  • Immunological phenomena — (Osler’s nodes, Roth spots)

Roth Spots.

Which part of the heart is affected by infective endocarditis?

  • Infective endocarditis affects the endocardium, especially the valves of the heart
  • Aortic valve is affected most frequently (aortic › mitral › right- sided valves)

Describe why bacteria typically target the valves in infective endocarditis.

  • The formation of a vegetation at the valves of the heart either results in changes to their thickness or a failure in their ability open and close appropriates
  • It is more common for bacteria to attach to the endocardium if underlying damage is present, and this occurs more frequently at sites of turbulent blood flow such as the valves of the heart

Describe how IV injections alter the risk of infective endocarditis.

  • Intravenous drug users are at increased risk of infective endocarditis due to repeated injection – potentially exposing their bloodstream to bacteria on the surface of the skin or use of non- sterile needles
  • Entry of bacteria into the blood stream is first and critical step in infective endocarditis. Increased risk of infective endocarditis is therefore seen in I.V. drug users, but also a complication of routine surgeries such as dental surgery
  • It is also more common in individuals that are immunosuppressed or have congenital heart defects leading to damaged endocardium

What is rheumatic fever?

  • Rheumatic fever is a temporary inflammatory condition caused by Group A streptococci infection
  • The organism affects connective tissue including in the heart and elsewhere in the body

What is rheumatic heart disease?

Rheumatic heart disease is a permanent condition as a sequela of previous rheumatic fever causing permanent damage to the heart valves
