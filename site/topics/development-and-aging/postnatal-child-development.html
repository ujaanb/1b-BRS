Overview

Describe the role of fetal genetics in prenatal development.

  • Minor effect overall
  • Maternal factors tend to override fetal genetic factors in determining prenatal growth

Describe the role of maternal genetics in prenatal development.

Maternal size important in determining birth size

Describe the role of paternal genetics in prenatal development.

Paternal genetic factors have little effect on birth

Describe the role of genetics in postnatal development.

  • Largely determines final adult height
  • Sex chromosomes have an effect: — XY boys are taller than XX girls

Describe the difference in the role of genetics in prenatal and postnatal development.

  • Prenatally, maternal genetics have a huge role
  • Postnatally, fetal genetics play the largest role

Recall the hormone that is most important for embryonic growth (in utero).

IGF-2

Recall the hormone that is most important for later fetal and infant growth (in utero).

IGF-1

Recall the hormone that is most important for postnatal growth.

Human growth hormone (hGH)

Describe the difference in the role of the endocrine system in prenatal and postnatal development.

Switch from IGF-2 → IGF-1 → human Growth Hormone (hGH)

Describe how nutrition is provided in prenatal development.

  • Placenta also controls hormones necessary for fetal growth
  • Maternal diet influences nutritional availability

What is the major factor that influences nutritional availability in prenatal development?

Placenta provides all nutrients to growing fetus, therefore essential for growth

Recall the most common cause of intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR).

Placental insufficiency most common cause of intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR)

Describe how varied nutrition provided in postnatal development can have different effects.

  • Adequate nutrition is essential for growth
  • Poor nutrition may delay the onset of puberty

Describe the significance of a lack of substrate availability in postnatal development.

Starvation due to lack of substrate availability can limit growth potential

Describe the significance of malabsorption in postnatal development.

Malabsorption of nutrients may cause reduced growth

Describe the significance of excessive nutrition in postnatal development.

Obesity occurs mostly as a result of excessive intake of food

Recall the two factors that play a part in the developmental environment of a fetus.

Uterine capacity and placental sufficiency important in providing optimal environment for fetus

Which of these two factors is more influential in fetal growth?

Placental function is more influential in fetal growth than uterine capacity

Recall three environmental factors that affect postnatal development.

  • Socioeconomic status
  • Chronic disease
  • Emotional status
  • Altitude (mediated by lower oxygen saturation levels)

Describe the proportional (size) relationship between the head and the rest of the body at birth.

  • 1/3rd
  • Head disproportionately large for the body ( 1/3rd vs 1/7th in adulthood ) at birth

Describe how growth velocity changes in the very early years of life.

Grows rapidly for the first 2 years, before slowing

Recall when the cranial sutures open.

Cranial sutures open at birth

Describe the significance of the cranial sutures opening.

List

Recall when the cranial sutures close.

Cranial sutures close by 18 months

Describe the significance of the cranial sutures closing.

List

Recall the four recognised phases of growth.

  • Fetal
  • Infantile
  • Childhood
  • Pubertal

Phases Of Growth.

Which of the four phases of growth is the fastest?

Fetal phase

What percentage of eventual height does the fetal phase account for?

~30%

Describe what drives growth in the fetal phase.

Growth mainly driven by hyperplasia during fetal life

How many rounds of cell division does a cell go through in the fetal phase of growth?

~42 cycles of cell division before birth

How many rounds of cell division does a cell go through after the fetal phase of growth?

Only further five cycles of cell division occur from birth to adulthood

When does the infantile phase last from and to?

0-18 months

What percentage of eventual height does the infantile phase account for?

15%

Describe the rate of growth in the infantile phase compared with the fetal phase.

  • Rapid, but decelerating growth (vs fetal phase) By what percentage does the length of a baby increase by in the infantile phase?
  • Length increases by 50%, head circumference by 30% and weight triples vs birth By what percentage does the head circumference of a baby increase by in the infantile phase?
  • 30% By what proportion does the weight of a baby increase by in the infantile phase?
  • Weight triples

What is the major factor that drives growth in the infantile phase?

Growth largely nutrition dependent

When does the childhood phase last from and to?

Covers 18 months to 12 years of age

What percentage of eventual height does the childhood phase account for?

~40%

Describe the rate of growth in the childhood phase.

  • Steady, slow prolonged growth By how much does the height of a child increase annually in the childhood phase?
  • 5-6 cm annual increase in height By how much does the weight of a child increase annually in the childhood phase?
  • 3-3.5kg annual increase in weight

Describe what drives growth during the childhood phase.

Good nutrition and health important, but endocrine growth regulation increasing

What is another name for the pubertal phase?

Pubertal growth spurt

What percentage of eventual height does the pubertal phase account for?

~15% of eventual height.

Describe what drives growth during the pubertal phase.

  • Rising levels of sex hormones boost hGH production By how much does the height of a boy (XY) increase annually in the pubertal phase?
  • ~25 cm By how much does the height of a girl (XX) increase annually in the pubertal phase?
  • ~20 cm

Describe a secondary effect of sex hormones during the pubertal phase.

Temporary growth spurt as sex hormones also cause fusion of growth plates, leaving an individual at their adult height

Describe the effect of the growth plates fusing.

An individual gets stuck at their adult height

Reproductive Hormone Changes Over Childhood.

What is mini-puberty?

  • Gonadotrophin secretion commences towards the end of the first trimester, peaks mid-pregnancy, then declines
  • Mini-puberty describes when the HPG axis is transiently activated

after birth, after release from restraint by placental hormones

Continues for around 6 months after birth before declining

Describe what triggers the activation of the HPG axis in mini-puberty.

Release from restraint by placental hormones (i.e removal from uterine environment)

Mini-Puberty.

In which gender is mini-puberty thought to be more important?

Males

Describe the function of mini-puberty in males.

Elevated sex steroids in males during mini-puberty seems to be important for normal gonadal development (testicular tissue and penile development)

Describe the function of mini-puberty in females.

Role of mini-puberty less clear in female infants — Oestradiol levels fluctuate through first few months after birth — Follicular development occurs in the ovary — Important for patterning and development of mammary tissue?

Describe how sex steroids during mini-puberty may impact body composition.

High testosterone levels in boys during minipuberty, may partly

explain the higher growth velocity observed in boys compared to girls What triggers puberty?

Control of puberty onset remains unclear, but influenced by metabolic status

Describe what drives the onset of puberty.

Release of neurokinin KNDy neurons may regulate release of Kisspeptin peptides, which act on GnRH neurons to promote pulsatile GnRH release

Describe a finding that supports this hypothesis.

Mutations in KISS1R affect puberty timing, implicating Kisspeptin- KISS1R signalling in regulation of this process.

What is consonance?

  • The developmental events of puberty typically follow a predictable pattern
  • Compliance with this sequence is known as consonance

What is menarche?

Menarche is the first occurrence of menstruation which often occurs in young girls undergoing puberty

Describe how the age of menarche is changing over time.

Age of menarche decreased by ~4 years betweenn1850-1960, then by a further 3 months per decade from 1977-2013

Stages Of Puberty.

Recall the four major non-physiological domains of child development.

  • Gross motor skills
  • Fine motor skills
  • Speech, language and hearing skills
  • Social/behavioural skills

Describe the major landmarks in the development of gross motor skills and the age at which they are typically achieved.

  • 6-8 weeks - raises head to 45º prone
  • 6-8 months - sits without support
  • 8-9 months - crawling
  • 10 months - cruises around furniture
  • 12 months - walks unsteadily, broad gait
  • 15 months - walks steadily

Gross Motor Skills.

Describe the major landmarks in the development of fine motor skills and the age at which they are typically achieved.

  • 6 weeks - follows moving object or face by turning the head
  • 4 months - reaches out for toys
  • 4-6 months - palmar grasp
  • 7 months - transfers toys from one hand to another
  • 10 months - mature pincer grip
  • 16-18 months - makes marks with a crayon
  • 14 months - 4 years - Towers being made — tower of three (18 months), tower of six (2 years), tower of eight or a train with four bricks (2.5 years), bridge (from a model) 3 years, steps (after demonstration - 4 years)
  • 2-5 years - draw shapes — line (2 years), circle (3 years), cross (3.5 years), square (4 years), triangle (5 years)

Fine Motor Skills.

Describe the major landmarks in the development of language skills and the age at which they are typically achieved.

  • Newborn - startles to loud noises
  • 3-4 months - vocalises alone or when spoken to, coos and laughs
  • 7 months - turns to soft sounds out of sight
  • 7-10 months - sounds used indiscriminately (to parents)
  • 12 months - two to three words other than ‘dada’ or ‘mama’
  • 18 months - 6-10 words, shows two parts of the body
  • 20-24 months - uses two or more words to make simple phrases
  • 2.5-3 years - talks constantly in 3-4 word sentences
