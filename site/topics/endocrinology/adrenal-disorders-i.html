Overview

What is a steroid hormone?

A steroid hormone is a steroid that acts as a hormone, derived from cholesterol

Recall the common precursor molecule for all corticosteroids.

Cholesterol

Recall the three broad classes of corticosteroid produced by the adrenal cortex.

  • Mineralocorticoids
  • Glucocorticoids
  • Sex steroids

Recall an example of a mineralocorticoid.

  • Aldosterone
  • NOTE: The name mineralocorticoid comes from the fact that these hormones regulate salt (minerals) and water balances

Recall an example of a glucocorticoid.

  • Cortisol
  • NOTE: The name glucocorticoid arises from the fact that these hormones increase blood glucose levels

Recall an example of a sex steroid.

  • Androgens
  • Oestrogens
  • NOTE: The name sex steroid arises from the fact that these hormones are involved in reproductive function

Which of the two has three layers, the adrenal cortex or adrenal medulla?

Adrenal cortex

What are these three layers, and what are their relative positions to each other?

  • Zona glomerulosa (outermost)
  • Zona fasciculata (middle)
  • Zona reticularis (innermost)

Recall the five enzymes activated by angiotensin II.

  • 21-hydroxlase
  • 11-hydroxylase
  • 18-hydroxylase
  • Side chain cleavage enzyme
  • 3-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase

Where in the body are these enzymes activated?

Adrenal glands

Recall the role of these five enzymes.

  • Aldosterone synthesis In summary, recall the three major effects of aldosterone on the body.
  • Restoration of blood pressure (low → high)
  • Sodium (Na+) reabsorption
  • Potassium (K+) excretion — Vasoconstriction too

Aldosterone And Cortisol Pathways.

Recall the five enzymes activated by ACTH.

  • 17-hydroxylase
  • 21-hydroxylase
  • 11-hydroxylase
  • Side chain cleavage enzyme
  • 3-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase
  • NOTE: All these enzymes are P450 oxidases except 3-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase

What is an enzyme?

  • An enzyme is a protein that catalyses a specific reaction
  • NOTE: In the synthesis of aldosterone and cortisol, specific enzymes catalyse the synthesis of particular alterations to the molecule

Describe the pathway to go from cholesterol to aldosterone.

  • Cholesterol → Progesterone
  • Progesterone → 11-deoxycorticosterone via 21-hydroxylase
  • 11-deoxycorticosterone → Corticosterone via 11-hydroxylase
  • Corticosterone → Aldosterone via 18-hydroxylase

Describe the pathway to go from cholesterol to cortisol.

  • Cholesterol → Progesterone
  • Progesterone → 17-OH progesterone via 17-hydroxylase
  • 17-OH progesterone → 11-deoxy deoxycortisol via 21-hydroxylase
  • 11-deoxy deoxycortisol → Cortisol via 11-hydroxylase

Aldosterone And Cortisol Pathways (2).

What is meant when cortisol is described to be a diurnal hormone?

This means it has varying patterns throughout the day, which repeat daily

Describe the diurnal variation of cortisol.

Cortisol is low at night, peaks in the morning and fluctuates throughout the day

Diurnal Variation Of Cortisol.

What is Addison’s disease?

  • This is primary adrenal gland failure
  • It leads to a lack of secretion of cortisol and aldosterone due to a dysfunction of the adrenal cortex

What causes Addison’s disease?

  • In the UK, it is usually an autoimmune condition against the adrenal cortex
  • Tuberculosis (TB) of the adrenal glands is also a common cause outside of the UK

Why do patients with Addison’s disease become tanned?

  • Due to adrenal insufficiency, cortisol levels in Addison’s decrease
  • The pituitary gland compensates by making large amounts of ACTH, in an attempt to increase cortisol levels
  • However, this also leads to an increase in Pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC) levels, as this is a large precursor protein for ACTH

When POMC is produced, it is cleaved to form smaller proteins such as ACTH, MSH (melanocyte stimulating hormone), endorphins and enkephalins

  • People who have pathologically high levels of ACTH due to Addison’s disease become tanned because there is simultaneously a high amount of MSH from POMC cleavage, which leads to darkening of the skin due to activation of melanocytes
  • NOTE: Autoimmune vitiligo may also co-exist with Addison’s disease

Recall three features of adrenal crisis.

  • Fever
  • Syncope
  • Convulsions
  • Hypoglycaemia
  • Hyponatraemia
  • Severe vomiting and diarrhoea

Recall a condition that may coexist with Addison’s disease.

Autoimmune vitiligo

Recall three consequences of Addison’s disease.

  • Cortisol deficiency
  • Aldosterone deficiency
  • Low glucose
  • Salt loss
  • High ACTH → Increased pigmentation
  • Low blood pressure
  • Eventual death
  • Atrophy of adrenal cortices

How is Addison’s disease treated?

Rehydrate with normal saline (salt) due to low salt

Give dextrose to prevent hypoglycaemia due to glucocorticoid deficiency

Fatigue

Recall three tests that can be done to diagnose Addison’s disease.

9am cortisol - LOW

Acth - High

What is the short synACTHen test?

  • The short synACTHen test, measures the ability of the adrenal glands to produce cortisol after being stimulated with synthetic ACTH — NOTE: synACTHen = synthetic ACTH
  • 250 micrograms of synACTHen is given to the patient and their cortisol response is measured

What is the treatment for primary adrenocortical failure?

  • The patient needs replacement of cortisol & aldosterone
  • There is no need to replace the adrenal sex steroids

Why do the adrenal sex steroids not need replacement in primary adrenocortical failure?

  • This is because the adrenal androgens play a minor role in adults
  • The gonads play the majority of the role in sex steroid production

How are aldosterone and cortisol replaced in primary adrenocortical failure?*

  • Hydrocortisone three times daily (10 + 5 + 2.5)
  • Prednisolone 2-4mg once daily — This would be an equivalent of 15-25 mg of hydrocortisone daily
  • Fludrocortisone (50 to 100 mcg) daily

Why can’t aldosterone be replaced directly?

It has a very short half-life, so it cannot be replaced safely via daily administration

What is fludrocortisone?

Fludrocortisone is simply aldosterone + one fluorine (F) atom and it is a mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) agonist

What is fludrocortisone used for?

Aldosterone replacement therapy

Why is fludrocortisone used?

  • The fluorine atom is added to make the aldosterone molecule non- biodegradable — This is because the fluorine atom means the enzyme that breaks down aldosterone can no longer break down fludrocortisone
  • This helps to increase the length of the fludrocortisone’s effects (long-acting)

What is the half-life of fludrocortisone?

  • The half life of fludrocortisone is 3.5 hours — However, its effects last for 18 hours
  • In contrast, the half-life of aldosterone is ~15 minutes

What are hydrocortisone and prednisolone used for?

Cortisol replacement therapy

How often is oral hydrocortisone taken?

  • Three times a day
  • Once a day administration leads to cortisol levels falling rapidly during the day. We want to mimic cortisol’s circadian rhythm

Cortisol When Administered Once A Day.

just give bigger doses?

We cannot just give bigger doses of hydrocortisone because this significantly suppresses the immune system

What is the best treatment for primary adrenocortical failure at the moment?

Prednisolone

Describe the difference between prednisolone and hydrocortisone.

  • Prednisolone has a double bond within its structure, which gives it a longer half-life, which allows it to be administered once daily — It is also more potent than cortisol
  • NOTE: Internationally, prednisolone has been approved for treating asthma, due to its immunosuppressive action

Prednisolone Vs. Hydrocortisone.

Recall the dose for prednisolone.

2-4 mg daily

What is congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH)?

Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) refers to a group of genetic disorders that cause primary adrenal failure

What causes congenital adrenal hyperplasia?

CAH is caused by mutations in genes responsible for producing enzymes necessary for hormone synthesis

Which enzyme is most commonly deficient in congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH)?

Most commonly, the 21-hydroxylase enzyme — This can be complete or partial

What is the commonest form of congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH)?

21-hydroxylase deficiency

Which hormones will there be a deficiency of in 21-hydroxylase deficiency sufferers?

  • Aldosterone
  • Cortisol

Which hormones will there be an excess of in 21-hydroxylase deficiency sufferers?

Sex steroids and testosterone

Why are the sex steroids in excess in patients with 21-hydroxylase deficiency?

Because instead of the precursors for aldosterone and cortisol being used to form the aforementioned hormones, they are all converted into sex steroids

How long can you survive for in 21-hydroxylase deficiency?

Less than 24 hours

How can you save a baby’s life in an acute presentation of 21-hydroxylase deficiency?

  • Intravenous (IV) saline At what age does complete 21-hydroxylase deficiency present?
  • Babies present within 1-3 weeks with a salt-losing Addisonian crisis

How do babies with complete 21-hydroxylase deficiency survive in utero?

  • Before birth, (while in utero), foetus gets steroids across placenta
  • Therefore, when they are born, cortisol and aldosterone levels fall, leading to the neonatal Addisonian crisis

Recall a clinical sign of complete 21-hydroxylase deficiency in girls.

  • Girls might have ambiguous genitalia — This is because they have been virilised by adrenal testosterone
  • If you see this, think of complete 21-hydroxylase deficiency

Describe the difference between complete and partial 21-hydroxylase deficiency.

  • In partial 21-hydroxylase deficiency, 21-hydroxylase is only slightly defective
  • In complete 21-hydroxylase deficiency, 21-hydroxylase is fully defective
  • Partial 21-hydroxylase deficiency means that there will be a bit of aldosterone and cortisol to get by with

Which is more common, partial or complete 21-hydroxylase deficiency?

  • Partial 21-hydroxylase deficiency At what age does partial 21-hydroxylase deficiency present?
  • Whenever it is picked up clinically
  • This is because these individuals are able to survive, unlike those with complete 21-hydroxylase deficiency

Recall a symptom of partial 21-hydroxylase deficiency in boys.

Precocious puberty — This is due to excess testosterone

Recall a symptom of partial 21-hydroxylase deficiency in girls.

  • Hirsutism
  • Virilisation

What is hirsutism?

Hirsutism is where women have thick, dark hair on their face, neck, chest, tummy, lower back, buttocks or thighs

What is virilisation?

Virilisation is when a female develops male characteristics or when a newborn has signs of male hormone exposure at birth

What happens in 11-hydroxylase deficiency?

The enzyme 11-hydroxylase becomes dysfunctional

Which hormones are deficient in 11-hydroxylase deficiency?

  • Cortisol
  • Aldosterone

Which hormones are in excess in 11-hydroxylase deficiency?

  • 11-deoxycorticosterone
  • Sex steroids
