Overview

What is meant when cortisol is described to be a diurnal hormone?

  • This means it has varying patterns throughout the day, which repeat daily
  • In this case cortisol is low at night, peaks in the morning and fluctuates throughout the day

Diurnal Variation Of Cortisol.

What is Cushing’s syndrome?

A syndrome caused by too much cortisol

Recall four clinical features of Cushing’s syndrome.

  • Red cheeks
  • Moon face
  • Fat pads (buffalo hump)
  • Easy bruising
  • Stretch marks
  • Poor wound healing
  • Muscle weakness (myopathy)
  • High blood pressure (due to poor glucose tolerance), Hypokalaemia
  • Thin skin
  • Mental changes (i.e depression)
  • Pendulous abdomen
  • Osteoporosis, compressed vertebrae
  • Diabetes

Why do patients with Cushing’s syndrome become very fat and bruise easily?

  • Cortisol has the effect of diverting protein production to fat production
  • This means patients with Cushing’s syndrome are unable to produce proteins, and all their energy is diverted to producing fat instead
  • This means they cannot produce proteins to repair damage, and their skin becomes very fatty

Recall four possible causes of Cushing’s (syndrome or disease).

  • Taking steroids by mouth — cortisol is a glucocorticoid
  • Pituitary producing too much ACTH
  • Ectopic ACTH (i.e in lung cancer, ACTH is produced by lung cells)
  • Adrenal adenoma/carcinoma that secretes cortisol

What is centripetal obesity?

Centripetal obesity is when a person has more fat concentrated around the centre of their body, such as their stomach and abdomen, than their extremities.

What is proximal myopathy?

  • Proximal myopathy refers to muscle weakness that primarily affects the proximal muscles
  • These are the muscles closest to the centre of the body, such as those in the shoulders, upper arms, hips, and thighs

What is 11b-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (11bHSD) 2?

  • 11b-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (11bHSD) 2 is an enzyme responsible for converting active cortisol into inactive cortisone
  • NOTE: 11β-HSD Type 1 (11β-HSD1) converts inactive cortisone into active cortisol in tissues.

Describe the role of 11b-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (11bHSD) 2.

  • 11b-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (11bHSD) 2 is used to protect the mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) from cortisol, ensuring that aldosterone is the primary ligand that acts at the receptor — It mediates active cortisol → inactive cortisone
  • Without 11β-HSD2, cortisol would overwhelm mineralocorticoid receptors (MR)

11B-Hydroxysteroid Dehydrogenase.

Recall two investigations that can be done to determine the cause of Cushing’s syndrome.

  • Blood diurnal cortisol levels
  • 24h urine collection for urinary free cortisol — NOTE: Cortisol usually highest at 9am, lowest at midnight
  • Low dose dexamethasone suppression test DIURNAL CORTISOL LEVELS IN CUSHING’S VS. PHYSIOLOGICAL.

What is dexamethasone?

  • Dexamethasone is an incredibly potent steroid that mimics the effects of naturally occurring steroids such as cortisol
  • Its long half-life allows for less frequent dosing compared to other corticosteroids like hydrocortisone or prednisolone — It is more potent than both

What is the low dose dexamethasone suppression test?

  • The low dose dexamethasone test is a diagnostic test that can help us to understand whether a patient has a problem with the amount of cortisol they are producing
  • Administering dexamethasone to normal patients will have effects that are similar to having high cortisol levels — This would induce the cessation of ACTH production from the anterior pituitary and hence cortisol levels would decrease to become very low
  • SUMMARY: A normal result shows suppressed cortisol levels, while a lack of suppression suggests Cushing’s syndrome.

What is a transsphenoidal hypophysectomy?

  • A transsphenoidal hypophysectomy is a surgical procedure that removes tumours from the pituitary gland
  • Hypophysis = pituitary

Describe how Cushing’s syndrome is generally diagnosed.

As more and more of the symptoms correlate with the blood tests, a diagnosis of Cushing’s can be safely made

Recall three drugs that can be used to treat Cushing’s syndrome.

  • Metyrapone
  • Ketoconazole
  • Osilidrostat

What does metyrapone do?

Metyrapone inhibits the enzyme 11-beta-hydroxylase

Describe the effects of metyrapone.

  • Since metyrapone inhibits the enzyme 11-beta-hydroxylase, it inhibits the biosynthesis of steroid hormones such as cortisol and so it can be used to treat Cushing’s syndrome
  • Steroid synthesis in the zona fasciculata [and reticularis] is arrested at the 11-deoxycortisol stage — NOTE: 11-deoxycortisol has no negative feedback effect on the hypothalamus and pituitary gland.

Recall two reasons that metyrapone is administered for patients.

  • Control of Cushing’s syndrome prior to surgery — Adjust dose (oral) according to cortisol — aim for mean serum cortisol 150-300 nmol/L — It improves patient’s symptoms and promotes better post- operative recovery
  • It can also be used for the control of Cushing’s symptoms after radiotherapy

Recall two adverse effects of metyrapone.

  • Hypertension on long-term administration
  • Hirsutism

Why does metyrapone lead to hypertension?

Metyrapone causes hypertension by increasing the levels of 11- deoxycorticosterone (DOC), a potent mineralocorticoid

Metyrapone.

What does ketoconazole do?

  • Ketoconazole blocks 17a hydroxylase, inhibiting cortisol production
  • NOTE: Its main use was as an antifungal agent – although withdrawn in 2013 due to risk of hepatotoxicity

What are the uses of ketoconazole?

Treatment and control of symptoms prior to pituitary surgery

Recall a side effect of ketoconazole.

  • Liver damage - possibly fatal
  • NOTE: When on ketoconazole, monitor liver function weekly, clinically and biochemically

Ketoconazole.

What does osilidrostat do?

Osilidrostat blocks 11b and 17a hydroxylase, inhibiting cortisol production

What are the uses of osilidrostat?

  • Similar to ketoconazole, osilidrostat is used in the treatment of Cushing’s
  • Osilidrostat is a new drug, currently used on a named patient basis

Describe an advantage of osilidrostat.

  • The inhibition of 11b hydroxylase as well as 17a hydroxylase prevents the build-up of 11-deoxycorticosterone → corticosterone which causes adverse effects such as hypertension
  • NOTE: Deoxycorticosterone may also increase, but not to a clinically significant level in most patients on therapeutic doses of osilodrostat

Osilidrostat.

Treatment chosen for Cushing’s syndrome depends on the cause

What is Conn’s syndrome?

A benign adrenal cortical tumour characterised by the adrenal glands producing too much aldosterone

What is another name for Conn’s syndrome?

Primary hyperaldosteronism

How is Conn’s syndrome diagnosed?

  • Renin - angiotensin system should be suppressed — This would exclude secondary hyperaldosteronism. For example, if the renin-angiotensin system was not suppressed and renin was, this would mean the renin was causing the hyperaldosteronism, which would make it secondary
  • NOTE: Renin induces aldosterone production

Recall three clinical signs of Conn’s syndrome (primary hyperaldosteronism).

  • Hyperaldosteronism
  • Hypertension
  • Hypernatraemia
  • Hypokalaemia

Recall two drugs that can be used to treat Conn’s syndrome.

  • Spironolactone
  • Epleronone

Describe the effects of spironolactone.

  • Spirolonolactone is a competitive antagonist of the mineralocorticoid receptor (MR)
  • It also blocks Na+ reabsorption and K+ excretion in the kidney tubules (potassium sparing diuretic)

What is the mineralocorticoid receptor (MR)?

  • The mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) is a receptor that mediates the physiological action of aldosterone and (less so) cortisol
  • NOTE: Aldosterone usually exerts its effects by binding to the mineralocorticoid receptor (MR)
