Overview

What is obesity?

A condition of abnormal or excessive fat accumulation in adipose tissue, to the extent that health is impaired

What is adipose tissue?

  • This is a specialised connective tissue primarily composed of fat cells called adipocytes that stores energy in the form of lipids
  • Adipose tissue is also known as body fat, and exists in different forms such as visceral fat or subcutaneous fat

Adipose Tissue.

Describe how BMI is used to classify obesity.

  • ‹18.5 : Underweight
  • 18.5 – 24.9 : Normal weight
  • 25.0 – 29.9 : Overweight
  • 30.0 – 34.9 : Obesity (Class I)
  • 35.0 – 39.9 : Obesity (Class II)
  • 40.0 and above : Extreme Obesity (Class III)

What is the main factor that drives obesity?

Genetics

Describe what twin studies have taught us about obesity.

  • Using twin studies we can assess phenotypic expression of obesity between identical twins and non-identical twins
  • Generally, identical twins (same DNA) tend to have very similar BMIs, however this similarity is much weaker in non-identical twins (half of DNA is shared)
  • Twin studies have hence taught us that obesity is largely genetic

Describe how genome-wide association studies (GWAS) work.

  • Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) are used to assess the influence of certain genes on the phenotypic expression of a condition
  • A control group and a group with the condition are assembled and DNA samples are obtained from all individuals
  • DNA is analysed for single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and differences between the two populations are observed
  • If certain SNPs are found significantly more often in cases than controls, these SNPs are considered associated with the disease or trait.

Describe what genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have taught us about obesity.

  • Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have taught us that there are several genes that are implicated in the phenotypic expression of obesity
  • These genes can increase one’s susceptibility to increased birthweight, body fat, BMI, chances of extreme obesity, waist-to- hip ratio and visceral adiposity

Where do most of these genetic changes have their effects?

  • Most of these genetic changes impact the brain, and how much we desire to eat
  • However from an evolutionary standpoint, it seems like the system is more inclined to prevent us from starving to death rather than prevent obesity

What is the main function of cholecystokinin (CCK)?

  • To stimulate pancreatic enzyme (i.e amylase, lipase) release from pancreatic acinar cells
  • To stimulate gallbladder contraction (bile release) and sphincter of Oddi relaxation

Recap: Fat Digestion.

Recall three other functions of cholecystokinin (CCK).

  • Delays gastric emptying (movement of acidic content from stomach to duodenum)
  • Decreases food intake and meal size
  • Trophic effects on the exocrine pancreas and gallbladder

What is the main function of glucose-dependent insulinotrophic peptide (GIP)?

It causes the secretion of insulin in response to meals

What is pancreatic polypeptide (PP)?

A hormone that has a potential role in inducing satiety

What does ghrelin do?

Also known as the “hunger hormone”, ghrelin levels increase when our stomachs are empty and when it is time to eat

What does leptin do?

Leptin is released by the subcutaneous fat to regulate (suppress) hunger and energy metabolism — It acts on the hypothalamus

What effect does peptide YY have?

  • Reduces intestinal motility, gallbladder contraction and pancreatic exocrine secretion
  • This makes sense as it is released in the distal portion of the gut - the terminal ileum, colon and rectum

Describe how the incidence of obesity has changed over the past few decades.

Obesity is increasing incredibly quickly over what is considered a short period of time in genetics

Recall five other factors that can be associated with the incidence of obesity.

  • Reduced ability to play outside
  • Increased car use
  • Increased screen time
  • Lower education level
  • Lower educational achievement
  • Poverty
  • Social deprivation
  • Overall, we are living in a more “obesogenic” environment now than ever before

Describe the interaction between environment and genetics in the phenotype of obesity.

  • Whilst genetics is thought to be the main driver of obesity, one’s environment can also play a huge impact
  • For example, an individuals in a very “obesogenic” environment are much more likely to have a higher BMI despite the fact they may have low genetic susceptibility

How many adults in the world were overweight in 2016?

  • › 1.9 billion adults were overweight in 2016 Of these, how many were obese?
  • Of these nearly 2 billion adults, ›650 million were obese

Recall seven comorbidities associated with obesity.

  • Depression — psychological impacts
  • Stroke — increased likelihood of vessel blockage
  • Sleep apnoea
  • Myocardial infarction — increased likelihood of coronary artery blockage
  • Hypertension — increased blood pressure caused by constricted vessels
  • Diabetes — reduced insulin sensitivity
  • Bowel cancer
  • Osteoarthritis — prolonged stress on joints
  • Peripheral vascular disease — increased likelihood of vessel blockage
  • Gout
  • Gallbladder disease
  • Infertility

Describe how obesity is associated with sleep apnoea.

Obesity leads to sleep apnoea generally via the narrowing of upper airway due to increased fat

Describe how obesity is associated with bowel cancer.

  • Ultra processed foods can have adverse effects on the bowels
  • Obesity causes a state of chronic, low-grade inflammation in the body, which can damage cells and increase the risk of cancerous changes in tissues, including the cells lining the colon and rectum.
  • High insulin levels → increased cell proliferation due to increased IGF-1

Describe how obesity is associated with gout.

  • High uric acid levels in blood is caused by high dietary purine intake
  • Purines are found in certain foods (i.e organ meats, red meats, alcohol, high fructose foods and seafood) and when uric acid levels in the blood are too high, it can lead to deposition of these uric acid crystals in joints, which is gout

Describe how obesity is associated with gallbladder disease.

  • Obesity generally increases the risk of gallstone formation within the gallbladder
  • People with obesity often have higher cholesterol levels in the liver,

which can lead to an excess of cholesterol in bile (the digestive fluid produced by the liver and stored in the gallbladder)

(see lecture diagram / notes)

When there is too much cholesterol in bile, it can crystallise and form gallstones.

In people with obesity, the gallbladder may not empty as effectively, causing bile to sit in the gallbladder longer (Gallbladder stasis) — This stagnation increases the risk of gallstone formation, as bile that is not regularly flushed out is more likely to form crystals

Describe how obesity is associated with infertility.

Obesity can disrupt the balance of reproductive hormones, particularly testosterone, and oestrogen as well as insulin and leptin

Describe how body mass index (BMI) is associated with mortality rates.

High BMI is associated with higher mortality rates

Bmi & Mortality Rate.

Describe how a reduction in obesity levels can help the population.

  • Reduction in general obesity levels and BMI can help to improve the economy and general public health
  • This is because the incidence of comorbidities such as diabetes and cardiovascular disease decreases which can have a big impact on a country

Describe the steps involved in the treatment and management of obesity in adults.

  • Determine degree of overweight or obesity
  • Assess lifestyle, comorbidities and willingness to change
  • Management — Lifestyle changes, drug treatment
  • Consider referral to specialist care
  • Specialist assessment and management — Surgery and follow up

Steps In Assessment And Management Of Obesity.

Describe how the treatment of obesity changes as BMI increases.

  • With individuals that have lower BMIs, advice is more centred around lifestyle and dietary changes
  • As BMI increases, there is greater focus on interventional techniques such as drugs and even surgery
  • Comorbidities can also increase the likelihood of prescribing drugs or surgery

Treatment Of Obesity.

Describe how dietary changes can aid in the treatment of obesity.

  • Eating a balanced diet that takes into account all of the food groups
  • Eating five different pieces of fruit and vegetables per day

What is leptin?

Leptin is a hormone produced primarily by fat cells (adipocytes) and plays a key role in regulating energy balance, appetite, and body weight

Describe how leptin levels may change.

Leptin acts on the hypothalamus in the brain to reduce hunger

When fat stores are sufficient, leptin levels rise, signalling to the brain that the body has enough energy, which reduces the desire to eat

NOTE: As body fat increases, leptin also increases

Recall three clinical features of leptin deficiency.

  • Infertility
  • Stunted linear growth
  • Decreased body temperature
  • Decreased energy expenditure
  • Decreased immune function

Recall three other physiological effects of leptin.

  • Regulates synthesis of thyroid hormones
  • Increases blood pressure
  • Activation of immune cells
  • Regulation of menstrual cycle
  • Regulates bone mass
  • Increased heart rate
  • Decreases glucose-stimulated insulin secretion

Action Of Leptin.

Describe how leptin administration can aid in the treatment of obesity.

Leptin administration can help increase satiety and feelings of fullness in leptin deficient individuals, however in those without leptin deficiency it is found to be fairly ineffective

Leptin Administration In Leptin Deficient Children.

Describe two effects leptin has in leptin deficient children.

  • Body weight can significantly decrease in leptin deficient children receiving recombinant leptin therapy — This is because all the physiological impacts of leptin were missing previously, but they become available once replaced
  • Leptin administration restores FSH/LH pulsatility in leptin deficient children

Describe the effects leptin has in women with hypothalamic amenorrhoea.

Leptin can also increase serum levels of Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH) and Luteinising Hormone (LH) in patients with congenital leptin deficiency after 12 months of leptin treatment

What is leptin resistance?

  • Leptin resistance is when the brain becomes less responsive to the signals leptin sends to stop eating and increase metabolism
  • NOTE: Having higher levels of leptin for a longer period makes you resistant to it

Describe the effects of high leptin compared to normal leptin levels.

  • High leptin levels are found in individuals with high body fat levels — i.e obese individuals
  • The leptin is frequently signalling to the brain to stop eating and increase metabolism, however the sensitivity to the signals are reduced and similar to insulin resistance in Type 2 diabetes, leptin resistance is often developed in obese individuals
  • This means that the effects of high levels of leptin often show little to no difference to having normal leptin levels
  • NOTE: This emphasises the fact that leptin is an anti-starvation hormone rather than an anti-obesity hormone

What is hyperphagia?

Polyphagia or hyperphagia is an abnormally strong, incessant sensation of hunger or desire to eat often leading to overeating

What is Orlistat?

  • Orlistat is an anti-obesity drug that is a gastric and pancreatic lipase inhibitor
  • It is a derivative of an endogenous lipstatin produced by Streptomyces toxytricini

Describe the effects that Orlistat has on the body.

Orlistat typically reduces dietary fat absorption by around 30% by nature of being a gastric and pancreatic lipase inhibitor

Recall two side effects of Orlistat.

  • Steatorrhoea (fatty stool)
  • Faecal incontinence

Describe how the effects of Orlistat on weight loss were assessed using placebo-controlled trials.

  • Meta-analysis of 11 placebo-controlled trials of 1 year in 6021 overweight or obese patients showed that Orlistat only reduced weight by 2.9%.
  • Due to this relatively insignificant effect on body weight, accompanied with the uncomfortable side effects such as fatty stool and faecal incontinence, drop-out rates were high and Orlistat is not a very popular drug choice for weight loss

What is glucagon-like-peptide (GLP-1)?

A hormone that is used to induce satiety, secreted in response to nutrients in the gut

What is the main physiological effect of GLP-1 and how does it do this?

It induces satiety by increasing the sensitivity of the beta-cells of the pancreas to glucose

Recall five physiological effects of GLP-1.

  • Enhances insulin secretion, reduces glucagon secretion
  • Improves insulin sensitivity
  • Increases satiety reduces appetite
