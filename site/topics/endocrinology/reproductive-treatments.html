Overview

How is low testosterone confirmed in men?

Confirm at least 2 low fasting measurements of serum testosterone in morning

Why must the testosterone measurements be fasting measurements?

Testosterone often decreases post-prandially

What time is testosterone usually measured?

In the morning — This is when testosterone levels are at their highest

Recall three symptoms of low testosterone.

  • Loss of early morning erections
  • Low libido
  • Decreased energy
  • Decreased shaving frequency

What is the treatment for low testosterone?

Low testosterone can be replaced using testosterone replacement therapy (TRT)

Describe the various methods of testosterone replacement therapy (TRT).

  • Daily gel — Care not to contaminate partner.
  • 3 weekly intramuscular (IM) injection
  • 3 monthly intramuscular (IM) injection
  • Less common (Implants, oral preparations)

Recall two safety monitoring measures taken in regards to testosterone replacement therapy (TRT).

  • Monitor haematocrit (risk of hyperviscosity and stroke) — This is because testosterone can induce erythropoiesis
  • Prostate specific antigen (PSA) levels — PSA can rise in prostate cancer, monitoring PSA helps us to assess effects of testosterone on prostate

What treatment should be given to males with low testosterone desiring fertility?

LH/FSH

Describe the effects of LH and FSH on the male gonads.

  • LH stimulates Leydig cells to increase intratesticular testosterone levels much higher than in circulation (x100).
  • FSH stimulates seminiferous tubule development and spermatogenesis

Why should we be careful not to give testosterone to men desiring fertility?

  • Giving testosterone treatment to men desiring fertility could further reduce LH / FSH and hence worsen spermatogenesis
  • This is due to the negative feedback mechanism provided by testosterone via the HPG axis — NOTE: Testosterone → Oestrogen via aromatase. Oestrogen induces negative feedback

Describe a way that spermatogenesis can be directly induced.

  • hCG injections — hCG acts on LH-receptors to induce testosterone production and testosterone can work with FSH to induce spermatogenesis
  • If there is no response after 6 months, then add FSH injections.

Recall the main treatment for anovulation in women.

Ovulation induction

Recall three clinical signs of polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS).

  • Hyperandrogenism (clinical or biochemical) — i.e hirsutism, acne, high testosterone
  • PCO morphology on ultrasound
  • Irregular periods

Polycystic Ovarian Syndrome (Pcos).

What is hypothalamic amenorrhoea?

  • Hypothalamic amenorrhea is a type of secondary amenorrhea caused by a disruption in the normal function of the hypothalamus
  • The body prioritises essential functions over reproduction in response to perceived stress or insufficient energy, effectively "shutting down" the reproductive system to conserve resources

Describe three characteristics of hypothalamic amenorrhoea.

  • Low body weight
  • Excessive exercise
  • Stress
  • Genetic susceptibility
  • Poor diet
  • Irregular periods

What is the aim of ovulation induction?

  • To develop one ovarian follicle
  • Generally, ovulation induction methods aim to cause a small increase in FSH

Describe the risks of ovulation induction.

  • If ›1 follicle develops, this risks multiple pregnancy — i.e twins / triplets
  • Multiple pregnancy has risks for mother and baby during pregnancy

Recall three pharmacological ways ovulation induction can be achieved.

  • Letrozole
  • Clomiphene
  • Gonadotrophins

Describe how Letrozole achieves ovulation induction.

  • Letrozole is an aromatase inhibitor
  • Aromatase is the enzyme that converts testosterone → oestrogen, so Letrozole reduces oestrogen levels
  • Since oestrogen is the hormone that primarily acts to down-regulate the HPG axis, decreasing oestrogen levels via Letrozole hence up- regulates the HPG-axis

Letrozole.

Describe how Clomiphene achieves ovulation induction.

  • Clomiphene is similar to Letrozole, but instead it works to block oestrogen receptors
  • This then inhibits oestrogen from down-regulating the HPG-axis

Clomiphene.

Describe how gonadotropins achieve ovulation induction.

FSH/LH injections (gonadotrophins) directly increase gonadotrophin levels in the blood

Gonadotrophins.

Recall three ovulation induction techniques for patients with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS).

  • Lifestyle / Weight Loss by 5%
  • Metformin — insulin resistance is a driver of PCOS
  • Letrozole (Aromatase inhibitor)
  • Clomiphene (Oestradiol receptor modulator)
  • FSH stimulation

Why is Metformin given to patients with PCOS?

  • Many women with PCOS have insulin resistance, so this needs to be treated alongside the PCOS — Metformin decreases the amount of glucose absorbed from food as well as hepatic glucose output (HGO) — It also increases the body's sensitivity to insulin
  • Both of these effects help to combat the effects of hyperinsulinaemia seen in women with PCOS

Recall three ovulation induction techniques for patients with hypothalamic amenorrhoea.

  • Lifestyle / Weight gain / reduce exercise
  • Pulsatile GnRH pump
  • FSH stimulation
  • Letrozole (Aromatase inhibitor)
  • Clomiphene (Oestradiol receptor modulator)
  • NOTE: Patients with hypothalamic amenorrhoea typically have low oestrogen anyways, so Letrozole and Clomiphene may be less effective here

What is the chance of conception over 1 year without Contraception?

85%

Recall a major treatment for infertility.

In-vitro fertilisation (IVF)

Frequency Of In-Vitro Fertilisation (Ivf).

Describe how in-vitro fertilisation (IVF) works.

  • Firstly, a large dose of FSH is administered to the woman’s ovaries in order to stimulate the development of multiple oocytes — The resulting oocytes are then retrieved
  • The oocytes are then left in a dish with sperm in vitro (IVF) — This is done either via in-vitro fertilisation (IVF) or intra- cytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI).
  • The embryos are then incubated and allowed to develop for a few days before being transferred back to the womb (endometrium)

What is intra-cytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI)?

This is when one sperm cell is directly infused with one oocyte to produce an embryo — On the other hand, IVF just involves leaving the sperm and egg in the dish, hoping that fertilisation occurs

Describe the benefit of intra-cytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI).

Since ICSI only requires one sperm cell, it is beneficial for those individuals who have a low sperm count

In-Vitro Fertilisation (Ivf).

What is the chance of conception over 1 year with fertility awareness / withdrawal?

25%

What percentage of pregnancies are unplanned?

19-30%

The combined oral contraceptive pill (OCP) is a form of hormonal birth control that contains artificial versions of both oestrogen and progesterone

What is the oral contraceptive pill (OCP)?

The oral contraceptive pill (OCP) is a form of hormonal contraception taken by mouth to prevent pregnancy

Describe how the oral contraceptive pill (OCP) works.

  • The oral contraceptive pill (OCP) generally works to inhibit ovulation
  • Oestrogen and progesterone are the two main hormones involved in the negative feedback mechanism (down-regulation) of the HPG axis, and this is what they do to reduce the overall chances of conception

Recall the three ways that oestrogen and progesterone work in the combined oral contraceptive pill (OCP) to enable contraception.

  • Decreased GnRH & LH/FSH release (oestrogen)
  • Thickening of cervical mucus (progesterone)
  • Thinning of endometrial lining to reduce implantation (progesterone) — NOTE: Even though natural progesterone thickens the endometrium to prepare it for pregnancy, progesterone within the combined oral contraceptive pill has different effects

Oral Contraceptive Pill (Ocp).

Recall four positives of the combined oral contraceptive pill (OCP).

  • Easy to take — one pill a day at any time of day
  • Effective
  • Doesn’t interrupt sex
  • Can take several packets back to back and avoid withdrawal bleeds — i.e choosing when to stop taking the pill → menstrual bleeding
  • Reduce endometrial and ovarian cancer
  • Weight neutral in 80% of cases — although 10% gain, 10% lose

Recall four negatives of the combined oral contraceptive pill (OCP).

  • It can be difficult to remember
  • No protection against STIs
  • P450 enzyme inducers (drug) may reduce efficacy — NOTE: Cytochrome P450 is an enzyme that increases liver metabolism. P450 enzyme inducers are substances used to increase the rate at which drugs are metabolised
  • Not the best choice during breast feeding — can reduce breastmilk production due to inhibitory effects on HPG axis and hence prolactin production

Recall three side effects of the combined oral contraceptive pill (OCP).

  • Spotting (bleeding in between periods)
  • Nausea
  • Sore breasts
  • Changes in mood or libido
  • Feeling more hungry
  • Extremely rare: Blood clots in the legs or lungs (2 in 10,000)
  • NOTE: Different pills can be tried out to see which suits the patient best

Recall three non-contraceptive uses of the combined oral contraceptive pill.

  • Helps make periods lighter and less painful — Endometriosis / Fibroids — Dysmenorrhoea (painful periods) — Menorrhagia (heavy periods)
  • Regular withdrawal bleeds / or no bleeds
  • PCOS treatment — OCP helps to reduce LH and hence hyperandrogenism (acne / hirsutism) seen in PCOS. It also increases sex-hormone binding globulin (SHBG) levels which help to reduce androgen levels by “mopping them up”

Describe how the combined oral contraceptive pill (OCP) helps to make periods lighter.

  • The synthetic hormones in the pill (oestrogen and progestin) prevent the endometrial lining from building up as it normally would in a natural menstrual cycle. — In a typical cycle without hormonal birth control, oestrogen helps thicken the endometrial lining to prepare for potential pregnancy, while progesterone stabilises it
  • With the pill, the combination of hormones keeps the lining thinner, as the body is not preparing for implantation
  • A thinner lining means there is less tissue to shed during the “period, ” resulting in lighter menstrual bleeding.
  • NOTE: The synthetic hormones within the pill have different effects to their native counterparts

Describe how the combined oral contraceptive pill (OCP) is taken.

The combined oral contraceptive pill (OCP) is usually taken daily in a 28-day cycle, following a 21/7 pattern

Describe what is meant by “withdrawal bleeds” for users of the oral contraceptive pill (OCP).

  • During the 21 days of active pills, the body receives synthetic oestrogen and progesterone, which keep hormone levels steady and suppress ovulation — These synthetic hormones also prevent the endometrial lining from thickening as much as it would in a natural cycle
  • In the last 7 days of the pill pack, no active hormones are taken (the "placebo week"). Without the continuous supply of hormones, oestrogen and progesterone levels drop sharply
  • This drop in hormone levels signals the endometrium (uterine lining) to shed, causing bleeding similar to menstruation

Describe how “withdrawal bleeds” can be beneficial for those on the oral contraceptive pill (OCP).

  • In those with irregular periods, this withdrawal bleeding can be useful to prevent endometrial cancer
  • This is because it is good for the endometrial lining to be replaced every now and then, otherwise a high risk of cancer is run

What is the difference between progesterone and progestin?

Progesterone is a naturally occurring hormone, while progestin is a synthetic chemical that mimics progesterone's effects

What is the progesterone-only pill (POP)?

The progesterone-only pill (POP) is a variation of the oral contraceptive pill that can be used for patients that cannot take oestrogen — i.e those with migraines, breast cancer, CVD, blood clots or smokers

Describe how the progesterone-only pill (POP) works.

The POP works primarily by altering the cervical mucus and, in some cases, inhibiting ovulation to prevent pregnancy

Recall three positives of the progesterone-only pill (POP).

  • Works as OCP but less reliably inhibits ovulation
  • Often suitable if can't take oestrogen
  • Easy to take — one pill per day with no break
  • It doesn’t interrupt sex

Can help heavy or painful periods

  • Periods may stop (temporarily)
  • Can be used when breastfeeding

Recall two negatives of the progesterone-only pill (POP).

(see lecture diagram / notes)

Can be difficult to remember

  • No protection against STIs
  • Shorter acting – needs to be taken at the same time each day

Recall three side effects of the progesterone-only pill (POP).

  • Irregular bleeding
  • Headaches
  • Sore breasts
  • Changes in mood
  • Changes in sex drive

Recall two Long-Acting Reversible Contraceptives (LARC).

  • Coils
  • Progestogen-only injectable contraceptives or subdermal implants

Recall three advantages of coils.

  • Suitable for most women
  • Prevent implantation of conceptus (embryo) — this is important for some religions

Can be used as emergency contraception Recall a disadvantage of coils.

Rarely, they can cause ectopic pregnancy

What are (intra-uterine) coils?

Contraceptive coils, also known as intrauterine devices (IUDs), are small, T-shaped devices inserted into the uterus to prevent pregnancy

Recall the two types of coil.

  • Intra-uterine devices — i.e copper coil
  • Intra-uterine systems — i.e Mirena coil - progestogen-secreting

What is the intra-uterine device (IUD)?

The intra-uterine device is an alternative name for the copper contraceptive coil

Describe how the intra-uterine device (IUD) works.

  • The copper IUD releases copper ions, which create an inhospitable environment for sperm.
  • The copper acts as a spermicide, reducing sperm motility and viability, preventing fertilisation.
  • Additionally, the IUD itself triggers an inflammatory response in the uterus, which is toxic to sperm.

How long does the intra-uterine device (IUD) last for?

5-10 years

What is an intra-uterine system (IUS)?

An intra-uterine system (IUS) is an alternative name for the hormonal contraceptive coil

Describe how intra-uterine systems (IUSs) work.

  • The intra-uterine system (IUS) secretes progesterone which thickens the cervical mucus and makes it less likely for sperm to reach the fallopian tubes — Synthetic progesterone is useful for thinning the lining of the endometrium during the menstrual cycle, which creates a less favourable environment for an embryo to grow
  • Therefore IUSs work both by thinning the endometrium and thickening cervical mucus

subdermal implants are usually administered?

  • These contraceptives are usually given to those who are not desiring fertility soon
  • They are a long-lasting treatment so may not be best option if desiring fertility soon

When can the copper intra-uterine device (IUD) be fitted?

The intra-uterine device (IUD) can be fitted up to 5 days after unprotected sex (‹1% chance of pregnancy)

Recall how long it usually takes for a sperm cell to fertilise an egg.

  • ~24 hours
  • NOTE: Once an egg is released via ovulation, it will survive up to 24 hours in the fallopian tube

Why can the copper intrauterine device (IUD) be fitted up to five days after unprotected sex when fertilisation usually occurs within 24 hours?

  • This is because the copper intrauterine device (IUD) works by preventing implantation, not just fertilisation
  • The copper is not only a spermicide, but it also makes the uterine lining inhospitable, preventing implantation in the case that fertilisation has already occurred

Contraceptive Coils.

Recall two forms of emergency contraception.

  • Copper intrauterine device (IUD)
  • Ulipristal acetate
  • Levonorgestrel

What type of emergency contraception are ulipristal acetate and levonorgestrel?

Morning-after pills

Describe how Ulipristal acetate works.

  • Ulipristal acetate stops native progesterone working normally and prevents ovulation — 1-2% can get pregnant if ovulation has already occurred.
  • NOTE: Native progesterone thickens the uterine lining in preparation for pregnancy.

What is Levonorgestrel?

Levonorgestrel is synthetic progesterone that is widely used in various hormonal contraceptives

Describe how Levonorgestrel works.

Synthetic progesterone within the pill prevents ovulation

Describe how Levonorgestrel must be used.

Levonorgestrel must be taken within 3 days of unprotected intercourse (1-3% failure rate)

Recall three side effects of the emergency contraceptive (morning-after) pills.

  • Headache
  • Abdominal pain
  • Nausea

Recall two important considerations to be made by those who take Levonorgestrel.

  • Liver P450 Enzyme inducer medications make it less effective
  • If a person vomits within 2-3hrs of taking it, they need to take another pill

Hormones Of The Menstrual Cycle.

Describe the difference between synthetic and native progesterone.

  • Synthetic progestins tend to have a more suppressive and thinning effect on the endometrium than native progesterone, maintaining a consistently thin, inactive lining
  • Native progesterone is known to thicken and prepare the uterine lining (endometrium) for pregnancy

Efficacy Of Different Types Of Contraception.

Describe three contraindications that can prevent one from using the oral contraceptive pill (OCP).

  • Migraine with aura
  • Smoking — (›15/day) at age ›35yrs
  • Stroke or CVD history
  • Current breast cancer

Recall three other considerations that may influence whether someone may or may not want to use the oral contraceptive pill (OCP).

  • Other conditions may benefit (be treated) from OCP — i.e menorrhagia / endometriosis / fibroids / PMS (Pre-Menstrual Syndrome) / acne or hirsutism
  • Need for prevention of STI transmission — use barrier methods instead
  • Concurrent medication — i.e P450 liver enzyme-inducing drugs reduce efficacy of OCP — Teratogenic drugs require more effective contraceptive measures
  • Ease of use — i.e may be difficult for some

What is a teratogen?

A teratogen is anything a person is exposed to or ingests during pregnancy that's known to cause fetal abnormalities

What is the menopause?

  • Menopause refers to the cessation of monthly menstruation - where ovaries release an egg for fertilisation
  • It can affect anyone who has periods and is a natural process, resulting in lower levels of circulating oestrogen and progesterone

Describe the difference between peri-menopausal, menopause and post- menopausal.

  • Peri-menopausal - Within the years leading up to menopause
  • Menopause - Time at 12 months after last menstrual period
  • Post-menopausal - After menopause

Recall two benefits of hormone replacement therapy (HRT) in menopausal women.

  • Relief of symptoms caused by low oestrogen
  • Reduction in osteoporosis related fractures

Recall three menopausal symptoms caused by low oestrogen.

  • Flushing
  • Sweats
  • Disturbed sleep
  • Joint pains
  • Brain fog
  • Decreased libido
  • Low mood

Recall three risks of hormone replacement therapy (HRT) in menopausal women.

  • Venous Thrombo-embolism (VTE) (orally) — This is because oral oestrogens undergo first pass metabolism in the liver which increases clotting factors
  • Hormone sensitive cancers — i.e breast/ovarian/endometrial cancer. The longer you are on HRT, the greater the risk of complications with cancer. — Assess risk in each individual before prescribing
  • Cardiovascular disease — this applies to older women, younger women at lower risk of CVD via oestrogen
  • Risk of stroke — Oral has more risk than transdermal oestrogens
  • Gallstones
  • Dementia

Oral Vs. Transdermal Oestrogens.

Recall one way the risk of endometrial cancer can be reduced in women on HRT.

Prescribe progestogens in all women with an endometrium — This helps to thin the endometrial lining during the menstrual cycle which will reverse endometrial hyperplasia and prevent the progression to cancer

What is a progestogen?

  • Progestogen is a general term for any substance, natural or synthetic, that has effects similar to progesterone, the natural hormone
  • Hence, progestogens can be an umbrella term for progestins and progesterone
