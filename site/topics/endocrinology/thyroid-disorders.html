Overview

Recall the three main causes of primary hyperthyroidism.

  • Grave’s disease
  • Toxic nodular thyroid disease
  • Viral thyroiditis

Describe how we can differentiate between Graves’ disease and other causes of primary hyperthyroidism.

  • Expophthalmos
  • Exophthalmos is pathognomonic to Graves’ disease
  • Antibodies are the main propagator of Graves’ disease (antibodies binding to TSH-R) and so they can often also bind to the muscles behind the eye

Pathognomy.

Describe how a scintigram works to assess thyroid function.

  • Radioactive tracer is given — usually technetium-99m (Tc-99m), either orally or via injection
  • Tracer accumulates in the thyroid — the thyroid tissue absorbs the tracer over time—this reflects the metabolic activity of different parts of the gland
  • A gamma camera scans the neck — a gamma camera is used to detect the radiation emitted by the tracer — it creates an image showing how much and where the tracer is concentrated in the thyroid

Describe how we can differentiate between Grave’s disease and toxic nodular thyroid disease on a scintigram.

  • Grave’s disease has a characteristic symmetrical, butterfly shape on a scintigram — This is because the entire thyroid gland is hyperactive
  • Toxic nodular thyroid disease shows spotted, nodular marks on scintigraphy — This is because the nodules are the only areas that are hyperactive

Why is propranolol the beta-blocker of choice to treat Grave’s?

  • Propranolol is non-selective and it also has effects which inhibit the conversion of T4 to T3
  • NOTE: T3 is more metabolically functional than T4

What type of surgery is given in Graves’ and why?

  • Complete thyroidectomy
  • In Graves’ patients who have partial thyroidectomy, hyperthyroidism is still observed, so a complete thyroidectomy is performed instead
  • These patients hence have to be on lifelong thyroxine replacement
