Overview

What is Type 1 Diabetes?

An autoimmune condition in which insulin-producing beta-cells in the pancreas are attacked and destroyed by the immune system

What happens in Type 1 Diabetes?

Insulin-producing beta-cells of the pancreas are attacked and destroyed by the immune system

What is the result of this beta-cell destruction?

The result is a partial or complete deficiency of insulin production,

which results in hyperglycaemia

The resultant hyperglycaemia requires life-long insulin treatment

Describe the difference between Type 1 and Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus.

  • Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune condition which involves the beta- cells of the pancreas being attacked and destroyed by the immune system
  • Type 2 diabetes is a problem with insulin resistance where the body’s cells become less responsive to insulin — Initially, the pancreas compensates by producing more insulin, but over time, insulin production may decline, leading to high blood sugar levels

Diabetes Classification.

What is meant by latent autoimmune diabetes in adults (LADA)?

  • Latent autoimmune diabetes in adults (LADA) is a form of autoimmune diabetes, similar to Type 1 diabetes
  • However, unlike typical Type 1 diabetes, LADA develops more slowly, and because it occurs in adults, it is often initially misdiagnosed as Type 2 diabetes

Describe the difference between latent autoimmune diabetes in adults (LADA) and Type 1 diabetes.

  • Type 1 diabetes is most commonly diagnosed in children and young adults, though it can occur at any age
  • LADA is typically diagnosed in adults over the age of 30 and it has a slower onset, though it can be diagnosed in younger adults as well
  • Both are autoimmune conditions which affect the beta cells of the pancreas

What is monogenic diabetes?

Monogenic diabetes is a rare type of diabetes caused by mutations in a single gene, distinguishing it from the more common types, Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes, which involve multiple genes and environmental factors

What is MODY?

  • Maturity-onset diabetes of the young (MODY) is a form of monogenic diabetes that typically arises due to a mutation in a single gene affecting insulin production or function
  • MODY follows an autosomal dominant inheritance pattern, meaning it is passed down through families and can be traced across generations.
  • NOTE: MODY usually manifests before age 25

What is mitochondrial diabetes?

Mitochondrial diabetes is a form of diabetes caused by mutations in mitochondrial DNA

Monogenic Diabetes.

When may Type 1 diabetes mellitus present?

  • Type 1 diabetes was traditionally thought to be a condition of childhood or early adulthood
  • However there is now good evidence that it can present throughout every decade of life
  • Clinicians are hence now faced with a challenge, trying to differentiate adult-onset type 1 diabetes from the much large numbers of cases of type 2 diabetes

Why is it difficult to differentiate between adult-onset Type 1 diabetes and Type 2 diabetes?

  • This is because Type 1 diabetes is increasingly presenting later on in life
  • Since Type 2 diabetes is commonly caused by a combination of environmental and genetic factors, it can be difficult to identify the cause of one’s diabetic symptoms

Age At Diabetes Diagnosis.

What is diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)?

  • Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) is a potentially life-threatening complication of diabetes mellitus that is caused by the excess production of ketone bodies
  • Since ketone bodies are acidic, this leads to a fall in the pH of the blood which can have devastating effects on the body

Which type of diabetes typically leads to diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)?

Type 1 diabetes

Explain why Type 1 diabetes leads to diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA).

  • In Type 1 diabetes, there is a decrease in the production of insulin due to beta cell impairment
  • Insulin is physiologically important due to its role in dictating the uptake of glucose into cells as well as suppressing lipolysis as energy is no longer needed after glucose uptake
  • In the insulin-deficient state induced by Type 1 diabetes, glucose is unable to be taken up into cells and used for energy, leading to hyperglycaemia
  • Since the glucose is unable to be taken up into cells and used for energy, this causes up-regulation of counter-regulatory hormones such as glucagon and lipolysis is induced to provide the energy that the body needs
  • Lipolysis occurs and triglycerides → glycerol + 3 NEFAs occurs
  • NEFAs can be further broken down into acetyl CoA via the beta- oxidation cycle
  • Physiologically, acetyl CoA is used in the Krebs Cycle to produce ATP, however due to the lack of glucose availability, ketone bodies are produced instead
  • A continuation of this leads to a decrease in blood pH and diabetic ketoacidosis

Explain why Type 2 diabetes does not typically lead to ketoacidosis.

  • Since Type 2 diabetes is typically characterised by insulin resistance rather than insulin deficiency, the body is still able to respond to hyperglycaemia to an extent
  • This means the little production of insulin and glucose uptake is enough to offset the excessive lipolysis → NEFAs → acetyl CoA → ketogenesis seen in Type 1 diabetes
  • However, DKA can still present in Type 2 diabetes

What is C-peptide?

C-peptide is a component of the prohormone pre-pro-insulin

Describe the importance of C-peptide clinically.

  • Insulin levels are very hard to measure, so instead C-peptide levels are measured.
  • C-peptide is a component of the prohormone pre-pro-insulin, so measuring C-peptide levels helps us to see how much insulin is being produced from the prohormone — However, we cannot use this measure in those who have insulin injections, as they do not have the prohormone

C-Peptide.

Describe a distinct diagnostic way we can identify Type 1 diabetes.

Pancreatic autoantibodies which are behind the beta-cell dysfunction in Type 1 diabetes can be measured

Describe the stages of development of Type 1 diabetes in terms of the immune system.

  • Starting point — Genetic risk (15x more at risk of type 1 diabetes)
  • Then, there is immune activation where the beta-cells are recognised as foreign and an attacked is induced
  • Then, there is an immune response which leads to the production of auto-antibodies against the pancreas

Immune Infiltration Of The Islets.

Describe why the immune system aetiology of diabetes mellitus is important.

  • This is because having one autoimmune condition can put you at high risk of other autoimmune conditions
  • Also, pancreatic autoantibodies can be useful clinically
  • Immune modulation also offers the possibility of novel treatments (we are not there yet)

Describe how the immune system is involved in the pathology of diabetes mellitus.

  • Antigen presenting cells (APCs) mistakenly present the pancreatic antigen to T cells as foreign
  • CD4 T cells identify this antigen and activate CD8 T cells
  • CD8 T cells lyse all beta cells with the antigen, and pro-inflammatory cytokines propagate the response
  • Also, impaired function of T-regulatory cells leads to a failure to suppress this autoimmunity

The Immune System In Type 1 Diabetes.

Are all beta-cells destroyed in Type 1 diabetes?

  • Some people with type 1 diabetes continue to produce small amounts of insulin
  • This is because not all beta-cells are always destroyed — However, this level of insulin is often not enough to negate the need for insulin therapy

Beta-Cell Levels In Type 1 Diabetes.

Which gene is mainly implicated in Type 1 diabetes?

  • HLA (Human Leukocyte Antigen) gene Are Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes examples of monogenic or polygenic conditions?
  • Polygenic — This means the expression of the conditions are impacted by the joint contribution of multiple genes

Genome-Wide Associations In Type 1 Diabetes.

Recall three environmental factors that can be implicated in the aetiology of Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus.

  • Enteroviral infections
  • Cow’s milk protein exposure
  • Seasonal variation
  • Changes in microbiota
  • NOTE: Since Type 1 diabetes is a polygenic disease, it can be avoided. This is unlike monogenic forms of diabetes such as MODY and mitochondrial diabetes which cannot be avoided

What is an enterovirus?

Enterovirus is a genus of positive-sense single-stranded RNA viruses associated with several human and mammalian diseases — Enteroviruses are named by their transmission-route through the intestine

What are pancreatic auto-antibodies?

These are the causative auto-antibodies behind Type 1 diabetes and are detectable in the sera (serum) of people with Type 1 diabetes at diagnosis

Describe the clinical importance of pancreatic auto-antibodies.

The presence of pancreatic auto-antibodies are now recommended for diagnosis in NICE guidelines — However, they are not necessary for diagnosis

Describe why the presence of pancreatic auto-antibodies is not necessary for diagnosis of Type 1 diabetes mellitus.

  • Generally, pancreatic auto-antibodies are only high in level at the point of Type 1 diabetes diagnosis
  • After a few years, the levels of antibodies can drop, as with any form of antibody, so they can be harder to detect — This is because the initial immune activation is further away
  • This means there may be cases of individuals with little to no levels of pancreatic auto-antibodies that still have Type 1 diabetes mellitus

Recall two pancreatic auto-antibodies.

  • Insulin antibodies (IAA)
  • Antibodies to Glutamic acid decarboxylase (GADA) — involved in GABA production
  • Insulinoma-associated-2 autoantibodies (IA-2)
  • Zinc-transporter 8 (ZnT8)

What is the difference between a symptom and a sign?

  • A symptom is a subjective manifestation of a disease or condition that is experienced and reported by the patient — It cannot be directly observed by a physician or measured objectively.
  • A sign is an objective manifestation of a disease or condition that can be observed, measured, or detected by a healthcare professional during an examination or through diagnostic tests.

Recall five symptoms of Type 1 diabetes mellitus.

  • Excessive urination (polyuria)
  • Nocturia
  • Excessive thirst (polydipsia)
  • Blurring of vision
  • Recurrent infections — i.e thrush
  • Weight loss — i.e due to muscle wasting
  • Fatigue

Recall five clinical signs of Type 1 diabetes mellitus.

  • Dehydration
  • Cachexia
  • Hyperventilation
  • Smell of ketones
  • Glycosuria
  • Ketonuria

What is cachexia?

Cachexia describes weight loss and muscle wasting that can’t be reversed simply by consuming more calories.

Explain why patients with diabetes frequently visit the toilet. Osmotic diuresis

  • Patients with diabetes are frequently hyperglycaemic which increases the amount of glucose that is excreted into urine by the kidneys
  • Since glucose is a solute, it draws water into the urine with it, which increases urine volume and diuresis

Recall three reasons why patients with diabetes often feel tired.

  • Dehydration
  • Psychological factors
  • Disrupted sleep — i.e nocturia, polyuria
  • Complications of diabetes — i.e neuropathy, kidney disease

Describe two ways that insulin directly decreases blood glucose levels.

  • Insulin increases rate of glycogenesis, producing glycogen so blood glucose can be stored in this form
  • It increases glucose uptake into the liver via GLUT4 transporter by facilitating movement of these intracellular transporters to cell membranes
  • NOTE: Glycogen is a storage form of glucose

Recall three other effects of insulin in metabolism independent of glucose.

  • Increased amino acid transport
  • Increased protein synthesis
  • Decreased lipolysis (we don’t need the energy)
  • Increased lipogenesis
  • It’s helping to build our energy reserves, hence why insulin is known as an anabolic hormone

Recall a clinical sign of ketoacidosis.

  • Fruity breath — This is due to acetone which is a ketone which is responsible for this smell
  • NOTE: There are three main types of ketone: acetoacetate, 3-beta- hydroxybutyrate and acetone

Describe the effects that insulin has on the liver, muscles and adipose tissue.

  • Decreased hepatic glucose output (HGO)
  • Decreased proteinolysis
  • Decreased lipolysis

Effects Of Insulin Deficiency.

Describe the effects that insulin deficiency has on the liver, muscles and adipose tissue.

  • Increased hepatic glucose output (HGO)
  • Increased proteinolysis
  • Increased lipolysis

Describe the impact that insulin has on ketogenesis.

  • Insulin reduces ketogenesis
  • This is because the body is able to access glucose and so lipolysis is reduced — This reduces NEFAs and hence acetyl CoA which is used to produce ketones

RECAP: How is glycerol used for gluconeogenesis?

Glycerol enters gluconeogenesis in the liver by conversion to glycerol-3-phosphate (via glycerol kinase), then to DHAP (via glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase), which feeds into the gluconeogenic pathway to form glucose.

Describe how Type 1 diabetes is treated.

Lifelong insulin therapy

Describe three aims of Type 1 diabetes treatment.

  • Maintain glucose levels without excessive hypoglycaemia
  • Restore a close to physiological insulin profile
  • Prevent acute metabolic decompensation
  • Prevent microvascular and macrovascular complications

Describe an acute complication of Type 1 diabetes.

  • Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)
  • Hypoglycaemia (secondary to treatment)

Recall two chronic microvascular complications of Type 1 diabetes.

  • Retinopathy
  • Neuropathy
  • Nephropathy

Describe two chronic macrovascular complications of Type 1 diabetes.

  • Ischaemic heart disease
  • Cerebrovascular disease
  • Peripheral vascular disease

Describe three components involved in the general management of Type 1 diabetes.

  • Insulin Treatment
  • Dietary support / structured educations
  • Technology — pumps and continuous glucose monitoring (CGM)
  • Transplantation
  • NOTE: Type 1 diabetes is a condition that is ‘self-managed’

Describe two unique characteristics of a physiological insulin profile.

  • Insulin is never completely suppressed
  • The prandial peak of insulin has two phases — i.e after eating, two surges of insulin occur to appropriately adjust blood sugar. This is to accommodate for how much food you have eaten

Physiological Insulin Profile.

Recall the two types of insulin involved in insulin therapy.

  • Short-acting (with meals) insulin
  • Long-acting (background) insulin

Why do we need long-acting insulin?

To ensure there is a basal level of insulin in the body throughout the 24 hours of the day — This can be seen in the physiological profile

Why do we need this basal level of insulin?

If there isn’t a basal level of insulin, there wouldn’t be inhibition of proteolysis, lipolysis and gluconeogenesis and acute complications such as diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) may present

Describe the difference between quick-acting and long-acting insulin.

  • Short-acting insulin mimics the natural insulin spike after meals (post-prandially)
  • Long-acting insulin provides basal insulin coverage throughout the day and night

Recall the two types of short-acting insulin.

  • Human insulin — exact molecular replicate of human insulin (actrapid)
  • Insulin analogue — Lispro, Aspart, Glulisine

Recall the two types of long-acting insulin.

  • Bound to zinc or protamine — Neutral Protamine Hagedorn, NPH
  • Insulin analogue — Glargine, Determir, Degludec

What is a bolus?

In medicine, a bolus is the administration of a discrete amount of medication, drug, or other compound within a specific time, generally 1–30 minutes, to raise its concentration in blood to an effective level — i.e short acting insulin

Describe the typical basal bolus regime for insulin daily.

  • Three times a day for short-acting insulin (bolus)
  • Once daily for long-acting insulin (basal)

Basal-Bolus Regime.

Describe how insulin pump therapy works to treat Type 1 diabetes mellitus.

Insulin pump therapy works by pumping short-acting insulin into the subcutaneous space of patients very frequently to negate the need for long-acting insulin — It can be programmed to give fixed amounts/hour — The user has to programme how much insulin to pump after a meal, requires a lot of patient input

Describe an advantage of insulin pump therapy.

Since long-acting insulin does not need to be administered, we can adjust the rate at which the pump is injecting insulin to alter levels of background insulin throughout the day

Describe a risk of insulin pump therapy.

  • If the pump malfunctions for a few minutes or hours, the patient will be left with little to no background insulin and their basal insulin will drop
  • This puts them at risk of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)

Insulin Profile From A Pump.

Describe how important dietary advice is in the management of Type 1 diabetes mellitus.

Dietary advice is integral to managing the condition of patients with Type 1 diabetes

Describe the nature of the dietary advice given to patients with Type 1 diabetes mellitus.

All people with type 1 diabetes should receive training for carbohydrate counting

Where possible, refined carbohydrate containing foods (sugary / high glycaemic index) should be replaced with complex carbohydrates (starchy / low glycaemic index)

NOTE: The glycemic index (GI) is a scale that measures how quickly a food raises blood sugar levels

Describe how a hybrid closed-loop pump (artificial pancreas) works.

  • A hybrid closed-loop pump (artificial pancreas) works by using a combination of a real-time continuous glucose sensor with an algorithm to use glucose value and calculate insulin requirement
  • The insulin pump is then able to administer calculated insulin doses based on requirements determined by the algorithm and the sensor

Hybrid Closed-Loop System.

Recall the two types of transplantation that can be undertaken in Type 1 diabetes mellitus.

  • Islet cell transplants
  • Simultaneous pancreas and kidney transplants

Describe how Islet transplants work.

  • Human islets are isolated from a deceased donor
  • They are then transplanted into the hepatic portal vein
  • It requires lifelong immunosuppression

Describe how simultaneous pancreas and kidney transplants work and why they are used.

Simultaneous pancreas and kidney transplants are undertaken because there is a better chance of survival of the pancreas graft

when it is transplanted with kidneys

It also requires lifelong immunosuppression

Recall two limitations of these transplantation methods.

  • Availability of donors
  • Complications of life-long immunosuppression

Describe how capillary blood glucose is monitored.

  • Continuous glucose monitoring — i.e using sensors
  • Capillary (finger prick) blood glucose monitoring — OLD
