Overview

Recall the surface area of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract.

~200m2

Describe the antigen load of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract.

  • The gastrointestinal (GI) tract has a massive antigen load
  • This is due to the resident microbiota (10^14 bacteria), dietary antigens and exposure to pathogens

What is meant by a “state of restrained activation”?

This describes how there needs to be a balance between immune tolerance and immunoreactivity within the gastrointestinal (GI) tract

Describe the role of the gut bacterial microbiota.

Bacterial microbiota are required for immune homeostasis of gut & development of a healthy immune system

Recall two things the gut microbiota needs to show tolerance to.

  • Food antigens
  • Commensal bacteria

Recall one thing the gut microbiota needs to show immunoreactivity to.

Pathogens

Tolerance Vs Immunoreactivity.

Describe the abundance of gut bacteria compared to our number of body cells.

There are 10^14 gut bacteria and 10^13 cells in the body — The gut microbiome is the most densely populated “ecosystem” on Earth

What are phyla?

Phyla (plural of phylum) are a group of organisms with a certain degree of morphological or developmental similarity

Recall the four major phyla of bacteria found in the GI tract.

  • Bacteroidetes
  • Firmicutes
  • Actinobacteria
  • Proteobacteria
  • NOTE: Viruses and fungi may also be present in the GI tract

Describe the roles of the gut microbiota.

  • To provide essential nutrients
  • Metabolism of indigestible compounds
  • Defence against colonisation of pathogens
  • It is effectively to provide traits that we have not evolved to provide on our own

Describe the difference between the genome of gut flora and our own genome.

The genome of gut flora is ~100 times our own genome

Recall two host factors that increase gut bacterial growth.

  • Ingested nutrients
  • Secreted nutrients
  • NOTE: Gut bacterial growth leads to increased bacterial cell numbers

Recall two host factors that increase gut bacterial lysis/elimination.

  • Chemical digestive factors
  • Peristalsis, contractions, defecation
  • NOTE: Gut bacterial lysis/elimination leads to decreased bacterial cell numbers

Describe the diversity of the microbiome in different gut regions.

Because each region of the GI tract has different conditions such as pH and different enzymes, different areas of the GI tract have different microbiota which are adapted to survive in different conditions

Gut Microbiota Diversity.

What is dysbiosis?

Dysbiosis is the general term for altered microbiota composition

What is symbiosis?

Symbiosis is the state of living together, but it doesn’t imply either partner benefits from the co-existence

What is a symbiont?

A symbiont is an organism that lives with a host without benefit or harm to either

What is a commensal?

A microorganism which benefits from association but has no known effects on host

What is a pathobiont?

A pathobiont is a symbiont that doesn’t normally elicit an inflammatory response but under particular conditions (usually environmental) has the potential to cause dysregulated inflammation & disease

Immunological Equilibrium/Dysequilibrium.

Describe three causes of dysbiosis.

  • Infection
  • Inflammation
  • Diet
  • Xenobiotics
  • Hygiene
  • Genetics

What are xenobiotics?

Xenobiotics are chemical substances that are foreign to living organisms — Xenobiotics are not naturally produced or found within the body

Describe how dysbiosis can affect the body in general.

  • Dysbiosis can affect several systems within the body including the brain, lung, liver, adipose tissue and intestines
  • It generally does this by causing inflammation in other areas of the body

Recall three bacterial metabolites/toxins that can affect the body negatively.

  • Trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) — implicated in cardiovascular disease
  • 4-Ethylphenyl sulfate (4-EPS) — implicated in neuroinflammatory disease
  • Short chain fatty acids (SCFAs)
  • Bile acids
  • Aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) ligands

Describe how dysbiosis can affect the brain.

  • Stress
  • Autism
  • Multiple sclerosis (MS)

Describe how dysbiosis can affect the lungs.

Asthma

Describe how dysbiosis can affect the liver.

  • Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)
  • Non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH)

Describe how dysbiosis can affect the adipose tissue.

  • Obesity
  • Metabolic disease

Describe how dysbiosis can affect the intestine.

  • IBD
  • Coeliac disease

Describe how dysbiosis can cause systemic disease.

  • Type 1 diabetes
  • Atherosclerosis
  • Rheumatoid arthritis

What is gnotiobiology?

  • Gnotobiology is the study of organisms raised in environments with known microbial communities or without microorganisms — i.e selective colonisation of germ-free animals
  • It can be used to study the effects of different microorganisms on hosts

Describe how an immunological defect affecting small intestine development would affect the Peyer’s patches.

Peyer’s patches would be fewer and less cellular

Describe how an immunological defect affecting small intestine development would affect the lamina propria.

The lamina propria would be thinner and less cellular

Describe how an immunological defect affecting small intestine development would affect the germinal centres.

The germinal centres would have fewer plasma cells

What are isolated lymphoid follicles?

Isolated lymphoid follicles (ILFs) are lymphoid structures in the gut- associated lymphoid tissue (GALT) that play a role in mucosal immunity

Describe how an immunological defect affecting small intestine development would affect the isolated lymphoid follicles.

The isolated lymphoid follicles would be smaller and less cellular

Describe how an immunological defect affecting CD8 T cells would affect the GI tract.

The CD8 cells would reduce in abundance and cytotoxicity

Describe how an immunological defect affecting CD4 T cells would affect the GI tract.
