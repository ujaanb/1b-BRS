What can cause high blood pressure in acute presentations?

Pain may cause high blood pressure as a reflex

What is the purpose of a nasogastric (NG) tube?

A nasogastric tube can have many purposes including: — Short-term nutritional support (usually liquid) — Medication delivery — Gastric decompression —

through removal of built up fluid, gas, ingested material or bile

What is the significance of the mesentery being involved in volvulus?

The involvement of the mesentery in volvulus suggests that there could potentially be ischaemia, due to the bowel blood supply being cut off — This leads to a high risk of perforation

What conservative management is effective in treating the majority of patients with a sigmoid volvulus?

  • A sigmoidoscope is passed with the patient lying in the left lateral position
  • A large, well lubricated, soft rubber rectal tube is passed along the sigmoidoscope
  • This usually untwists the volvulus, with release of vast quantities of flatus & liquid faeces

What is the risk of sigmoid volvulus remaining untreated?

Left untreated, the loop of sigmoid, with its blood supply cut off by the torsion, would undergo necrosis

What is ileus?

Ileus refers to a painful obstruction of the ileum or any part of the intestine

What type of imaging would you do for suspected acute mesenteric ischaemia and why?

CT angiogram — May demonstrate thrombus in the mesenteric arteries & veins — Abnormal enhancement of bowel wall — Presence of embolus or infarction of other organs

What should you always do to initially manage a patient that present with an acute abdomen?

ABCDE approach

Describe how exploratory laparotomy is used in the management of acute abdomen. Exploratory Laparotomy

  • Midline incision
  • Evaluate the abdominal viscera
  • If obvious intestinal necrosis – resection of the affected bowel loops Damage control laparotomy
  • Stapled off bowel ends may be left in discontinuity
  • Re-inspect after a period of continued ICU resuscitation to restore physiological balance

Describe how blood flow can be restored to the superior mesenteric artery (SMA)

  • Embolectomy of SMA – in embolic AMI
  • Endovascular management of SMA thrombus – in thrombotic AMI
  • Arterial bypass of SMA - in thrombotic AMI

Recall the three groups of causes of acute mesenteric ischaemia.

  • Embolic (50%)
  • Thrombotic (20-35%)
  • Non-occlusive (‹5%)

Recall three embolic causes of acute mesenteric ischaemia.

  • From left auricle - atrial fibrillation
  • A mural infarct — blood clot in the wall of a blood vessel or heart chamber
  • Atheroma from aorta or aneurysm
  • Endocarditis vegetations
  • Left atrial myxoma — tumour

Recall three thrombotic causes of acute mesenteric ischaemia.

  • Blocks origin of superior mesenteric artery & can cause ischaemia of full length of small bowel
  • Due to atherosclerosis — Often all main splanchnic vessels—coeliac, superior & inferior mesenteric arteries

Recall three non-occlusive causes of acute mesenteric ischaemia.

  • Due to hypotension/hypoperfusion.
  • Due to vasospasm in shock — nonocclusive mesenteric ischaemia (NOMI).
  • Critically ill patients with vasopressor requirements — vasopressors constrict blood vessels
  • Those undergoing dialysis with large volume fluid removal

Recall three venous causes of acute mesenteric ischaemia. Superior mesenteric vein thrombosis

  • Occurs in patients with: — Portal hypertension — Portal pyaemia — Sickle cell disease
  • All of these causes are related to the presence of an underlying hypercoagulable state which can in turn lead to acute mesenteric ischaemia

Describe how superior mesenteric vein thrombosis can lead to

acute mesenteric ischaemia. • The superior mesenteric vein drains blood from the small intestine, caecum, and ascending colon, and joins with the splenic vein to form the portal vein ◦ Portal vein → Hepatic veins → Inferior vena cava • Normally, this allows deoxygenated blood to leave the bowel, maintaining healthy circulation. However the hypercoagulable state prevents physiological blood flow at healthy levels, reducing blood flow

through arteries and capillaries

What is portal pyaemia?

  • Also known as pylephlebitis, this is a form of septic (often suppurative) thrombophlebitis of the portal venous system
  • It is a complication of intra-abdominal sepsis: — Diverticulitis — Appendicitis
