Overview

What is benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV)?

  • Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) is a common inner ear disorder that causes brief episodes of intense vertigo (a sensation of spinning or movement) when the head is moved in certain positions
  • NOTE: BPPV is considered “benign” because it is not life-threatening, "paroxysmal" because it comes and goes in sudden bursts, and “positional” because it is triggered by changes in head position

Describe the mechanism behind benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV).

  • BPPV occurs when tiny calcium carbonate crystals, known as otoconia or otoliths, become dislodged from the utricle or saccule in the inner ear
  • These crystals usually sit on the macula of the utricle, where they help sense linear movements like forward or backward acceleration. In BPPV, however, some of these crystals dislodge and move into one of the semicircular canals — This is problematic because the semicircular canals are sensitive to rotational movements, not linear acceleration.

When the head changes position, the dislodged otoconia move within the semicircular canal fluid ( endolymph ), causing the fluid to flow abnormally

  • This flow stimulates the hair cells in the ampulla of the canal, falsely signalling to the brain that there is head movement when there isn’t
  • The result is a sensation of spinning or vertigo

Describe why benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) involves intermittent attacks.

  • BPPV is a peripheral disorder where the otoliths (carbon crystals) from the utricle, detach from the maculae and float around the semi- circular canals
  • Otoliths are not supposed to be found in the canals and therefore when these are floating in the canals, will induce a bigger endolymph flow

when the head moves (the crystals will carry more liquid) and therefore the movement of the head would be processed by the brain as bigger and faster than it is in reality

What is the pharyngotympanic tube?

  • A tube that connects the middle ear cavity with the nasopharynx
  • It clears mucus from the middle ear into the nasopharynx
  • It also equalises the pressure between the middle ear and the atmosphere

What is the function of the Eustachian (pharyngotympanic) tube?

It aerates the middle ear system and clears mucus from the middle ear into the nasopharynx

Does BPPV cause hearing problems?

  • This disorder only affects the semi-circular canals and not in a permanent manner
  • No hearing deficits are associated with this particular disorder, so any problem with hearing will not have been triggered by, or during, the period of this disorder (if a person has a hearing loss as the patient of this case, they will refer to hearing loss, but it is clearly not related to the vestibular event

How can hearing and balance problems be related?

  • In many conditions (and because the inner ear participates in the transduction of sound and head movement) hearing loss and vestibular symptoms are part of the same condition and the presence of both symptoms will definitely point to an inner ear dysfunction
  • So, in many conditions, the hearing problem and the dizziness are related. However, in this case, BPPV affects only the semi-circular canals where the crystals are floating around, then the hearing would not be affected
  • NOTE: It is possible for hearing and balance problems to co-exist, but consider the case to see if they are related. Many older patients suffer from age-related hearing loss.
