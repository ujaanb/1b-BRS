Overview

What is inflammation of the spinal cord known as?

  • Myelitis
  • NOTE: Inflammation of the spinal cord is known as myelitis (Greek “ marrow ”). When both the brain and spinal cord are involved, the condition is known as encephalomyelitis

What types of infections can cause encephalitis and meningitis? Encephalitis

  • Viral — Herpes Simplex Virus — Measles — Varicella (Chickenpox) — Rubella (German measles) Meningitis
  • Bacterial — Pneumococcal - streptococcus pneumoniae — Meningococcal - Neisseria meningitides — Haemophilia Influenzae B (HiB) — reduced by Hib vaccination, previously a leading cause in children under 5 — Streptococcal
  • NOTE: Meningitis is caused more commonly by bacteria which initially cause an upper respiratory tract infection and then travel

through the blood to the brain Which of the causative agents of meningitis is most serious?

Pneumococcal

What are some hallmarks of encephalitis and meningitis? Encephalitis

  • Fever
  • Seizures
  • Change in behaviour
  • Confusion and disorientation Meningitis
  • Sudden fever
  • Severe headache
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Double vision
  • Drowsiness
  • Sensitivity to bright light (photophobia)
  • Stiff neck
  • Rash (not always)

Describe the main differences in the signs of encephalitis and meningitis.

  • Encephalitis usually always has mental alteration, meningitis less commonly does
  • Encephalitis has seizures quite often, meningitis less commonly does
  • Focal neurological findings are a hallmark feature of encephalitis, meningitis less commonly has these — i.e weakness, visual disturbance, aphasia, cerebellar findings, behaviour change
  • Meningitis is more commonly linked with photophobia, encephalitis less commonly is
  • Meningitis is more commonly linked with stiff neck/rash, encephalitis less commonly is

What diagnostic tests can be performed? What might be found on these tests?

  • Neurological examination
  • CT
  • MRI
  • Lumbar puncture — CSF is usually clear and colourless — Raised white blood cell counts are a sign of inflammation → cloudy CSF — Low glucose in bacterial meningitis (bacteria consume glucose)
  • Blood
  • Urine analysis

What treatment options are commonly used?

  • Antibiotics
  • Antivirals
  • Corticosteroids (reduces inflammation)
  • Immune suppressors

What are some of the long term effects of meningitis?

  • Long term learning disabilities — cerebral cortex
  • Memory loss — cerebral cortex
  • Poor concentration — cerebral cortex
  • Clumsiness/co-ordination problems
  • Headaches
  • Deafness/hearing problems/tinnitus/dizziness/loss of balance — vestibulocochlear system
  • Epilepsy
  • Weakness/paralysis/spasms
  • Speech problems
  • Visual problems — raised ICP → papilloedema → optic nerve damage.
