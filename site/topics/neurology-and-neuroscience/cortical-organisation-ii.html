Overview

What is multiple sclerosis (MS)?

Multiple sclerosis (MS) is an autoimmune disorder which results in the loss of myelin from neurons of the central nervous system — i.e. the brain and spinal cord

Recall three symptoms of multiple sclerosis (MS).

  • Blurred vision
  • Fatigue
  • Difficulty walking
  • Numbness or tingling (paraesthesia) in different parts of the body
  • Muscle stiffness and spasms

What is peripheral nerve stimulation?

This is a technique that involves delivering an electrical signal to the peripheral nerves — i.e those outside the brain and spinal cord

Describe how transmission of nervous impulses occurs in the reflex arc, using the patellar tendon reflex as an example.

  • Tendon hammer hits the tendon in the kneecap, causing the tendon and hence the muscles in the thigh (quadriceps) to stretch as they are pulled down
  • This change in muscle length stimulates the sensory receptors in the muscles (muscle spindles) and signal is sent from this sensory neuron from the muscle — A sensory signal is sent from the muscle to the brain, allowing us to feel the hammer — A sensory signal is sent from the muscle to the spinal cord, synapsing with a motor neuron which tells the muscles in the thigh to contract again after they have been hit to return their length back to normal (the reflex)
  • This quadricep contraction leads to movement of the lower leg
  • NOTE: Muscle spindles are sensory receptors within skeletal muscles that detect changes in muscle length and the rate of change in length
  • SUMMARY: Stimulus → Sensory nerve activation → Transmission to brain and spinal motor neuron → Activation of motor neuron → Muscle contraction

Peripheral Nerve Stimulation.

Describe how peripheral nerve stimulation works.

  • An electrical stimulus of an appropriate intensity to a peripheral nerve can activate both sensory and motor axons
  • The activation of the motor axons via electrical stimuli can cause action potentials to travel along the nerve to cause muscle contraction, a twitch — This twitch can be recorded with electromyography (EMG)
  • NOTE: Electromyography (EMG) assesses the electrical activity of muscles and the nerves that control them

What is the M-wave in peripheral nerve stimulation and what happens in it?

The M-wave refers to the wave recorded on an electromyogram that is caused by the activation of the motor axons via electrical stimuli

The M-Wave.

What is the stimulus artefact?

  • The stimulus artefact is the initial spike or deflection seen on an electromyogram (EMG)
  • It is caused by the electrical stimulus itself, not by the nerve or muscle response — It helps us to mark the exact moment of stimulation

Describe what happens in the H-reflex in peripheral nerve stimulation.

  • Instead, of travelling directly to the muscle as in the M-wave, the action potentials can travel along the sensory nerve to the spinal cord
  • These can then cause the lower motor neurons in the spinal cord to become activated — Sensory neurones synapse with lower motor neurons
  • Action potentials in these motor axons can then travel along the motor neuron to the muscle where they cause muscle contraction, a twitch
  • This is a reflex activation of the muscle and is known as the H-reflex (after Paul Hoffmann, who first described it)

The H-Reflex.

What is the F-wave in peripheral nerve stimulation and what happens in it?

  • A large electrical stimulus can cause activation of the motor axons to conduct antidromically
  • These action potentials travel along the motor nerve to the spinal cord — i.e. in the opposite way to normal
  • These can then cause the lower motor neurons in the spinal cord to become activated
  • Action potentials in the motor axons can travel along the motor neuron to the muscle where they cause muscle contraction, a twitch
  • This later response is called the F-wave, and it occurs after the M- wave, which is still produced by this supramaximal stimulus — some signal goes directly to muscle (M-wave), some goes backwards and comes back to muscle (F-wave)

What does the “F” in F-wave mean?

  • This later response is called the F-wave, as it was first demonstrated in the foot Is the F-wave a reflex? Why?
  • The F-wave is not a reflex
  • This is because it doesn’t involve an afferent response to a stimulus conducted by sensory neurons and receptors, but it is the result of direct electrical stimulation of a motor neuron

The F-Wave.

What is meaning of the word orthodromic?

Travelling in the normal direction in a nerve fibre

What is meaning of the word antidromic?

Travelling in the opposite direction to that normal in a nerve fibre

Describe an example of a situation where we may see an H-reflex without an M-wave.

Direct sensory neuron stimulation — Here, the electrical impulse will not travel directly down the motor neurone to the muscle (as seen in the M-wave), but instead will take the sensory pathway and cause the H-reflex

What is a motor evoked potential (MEP)?

A motor evoked potential (MEP) describes the response seen on an electromyogram after the primary motor cortex is stimulated and leads to muscle contraction

What is the purpose of transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS)?

  • It is a technique that uses electromagnetic induction to stimulate neurons which basically tests whether or not they are intact
  • To assess the functional integrity of neural circuits

Describe how transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) can lead to a motor evoked potential (MEP).

  • Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) can induce stimulation of the primary motor cortex which is the origin of upper motor neurons
  • The activation of the upper motor neurons causes a ction potentials to travel along the entire motor pathway ( upper and lower motor neurons) to cause muscle contraction
  • The corresponding EMG response is known as a motor evoked potential (MEP)
  • NOTE: This is an example of central nerve stimulation, in contrast with peripheral nerve stimulation

What is total motor conduction time (TMCT)?

This is the time it takes for an electrical signal to travel from the brain to the muscle — i.e from stimulation to contraction, the whole thing
