Overview

What does the word “palsy” mean?

Palsy refers to various types of paralysis or weakness, often accompanied by loss of sensation and involuntary movements like shaking

Recall the motor innervations (roles) of the oculomotor nerve (CN III).

  • Superior rectus
  • Medial rectus
  • Inferior rectus
  • Inferior oblique

Recall the somatic innervations (roles) of the oculomotor nerve (CN III).

Levator palpebrae superioris

Recall the parasympathetic innervations (roles) of the oculomotor nerve (CN III).

  • Ciliary muscle (accommodation)
  • Constrictor (sphincter) of the iris

Describe the difference between the sphincter pupillae and the dilator pupillae.

  • Sphincter pupillae causes the pupil to constrict when it contracts
  • Dilator pupillae causes the pupil to dilate when it contracts

Sphincter Pupillae Vs. Dilator Pupillae.

Describe what happens when the ciliary muscles contract and relax.

(see lecture diagram / notes)

When the ciliary muscles contract, this causes the zonular fibres (suspensory ligaments) to relax which leads to rounding of the lens, increasing its refractive power

When the ciliary muscles relax, this causes the zonular fibres (suspensory ligaments) to tighten which leads to flattening of the lens, decreasing its refractive power

Oculomotor Nerve In The Efferent Visual Pathway.

Which pathologies may cause oculomotor nerve (CN III) lesions?

  • Lesions affecting the oculomotor nerve are usually classified into medical and surgical Medical lesions
  • Typically affecting the vasculature to the nerve (and hence the central portion of the nerve), tend to not affect the pupil (‘pupil sparing’) as the parasympathetic fibres running to the eye are in the outer portion of the nerve
  • Microvascular disease due to hypertension, diabetes mellitus Surgical lesions
  • Usually in the form of a posterior communicating artery aneurysm, tend to affect the nerve. The aneurysm typically compresses the outer portion of the nerve fibres, which is where the parasympathetic nerves run
  • In relation to the Circle of Willis: CN3 runs in between the posterior cerebral and the superior cerebellar artery

Which blood vessel is the oculomotor nerve closely associated with which can cause oculomotor pathology?

  • Posterior communicating artery (PCA)
  • Aneurysm of this artery can lead to compression of the oculomotor nerve

Which two blood vessels does CN3 run in between in the Circle of Willis?

CN3 runs in between the posterior cerebral and the superior cerebellar artery

Where are the parasympathetic fibres of the oculomotor nerve located?

On the outer portion of the nerve (peripherally)

Describe when parasympathetic function of the oculomotor nerve is impaired by lesions.

  • Usually with surgical lesions — i.e those that compress the nerve
  • This is because the parasympathetic fibres of the oculomotor nerve (CN III) are located peripherally (on the outer portion of the nerve)
  • Hence, medical lesions such as diabetes-induced microvascular disease would usually only affect the vasculature to the nerve, hence the central portion and will be pupil sparing

Why is the periphery of the oculomotor nerve spared by diabetic ophthalmoplegia?

The outer portion of CN III (and hence the parasympathetic fibres) have a more robust blood supply to them from pial vessels, whereas the inner portion is supplied by the less robust vasa nervorum, which are more susceptible to microvascular damage from diabetes
