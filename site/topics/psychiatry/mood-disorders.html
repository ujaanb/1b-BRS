Overview

What is bipolar disorder?

Bipolar disorder is a mental health condition characterised by significant mood swings between periods of intense elevation (mania or hypomania) and periods of depression

Recall the two types of bipolar disorder.

  • Bipolar I
  • Bipolar II

1.1%

What is a depressive episode?

A depressive episode is a period of time when a person experiences symptoms of depression

Describe what needs to be met for a depressive episode to be diagnosed.

Occurrence of 2 weeks or more of depressed mood AND the presence of 4 of 8 out of the following: — Sleep alterations (insomnia or hypersomnia) — Appetite alterations (increased or decreased) — Diminished interest or anhedonia — Decreased concentration — Low energy — Guilt — Psychomotor changes (agitation or retardation) — Suicidal thoughts

Recall the eight DSM-5 criteria for depressive episodes.

  • Sleep alterations (insomnia or hypersomnia)
  • Appetite alterations (increased or decreased)
  • Diminished interest or anhedonia
  • Decreased concentration
  • Low energy
  • Guilt
  • Psychomotor changes (agitation or retardation)
  • Suicidal thoughts

Why is anhedonia, low energy and depressed mood in bold above?

These are the core symptoms of depression

Describe when major depressive disorder (MDD) can be suggested as a diagnosis.

If no manic or hypomanic episodes in the past are identified, then the diagnosis of a current major depressive episode leads to a longitudinal diagnosis of Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)

Atypical features —

  • Melancholic features — defined by no mood reactivity, along with marked psychomotor retardation and anhedonia
  • Psychotic features — the presence of delusions/hallucinations

Cycle Of High Mood.

What is mania?

Mania is a psychiatric behavioural syndrome defined as a state of abnormally elevated arousal, affect, and energy level

What is a manic episode?

This is a period of abnormally elevated arousal, affect, and energy level

Describe the criteria that needs to be met in order to make a diagnosis of manic episode.

Euphoric or irritable mood with 3 or more of 7 manic criteria: — Decreased need for sleep with increased energy — Distractibility — Grandiosity or inflated self-esteem — Flight of ideas or racing thoughts — Increased talkativeness or pressured speech — Increased goal-directed activities or psychomotor agitation — Impulsive behaviour (such as sexual impulsivity or spending sprees)

Recall the seven manic criteria.

  • Decreased need for sleep with increased energy
  • Distractibility
  • Grandiosity or inflated self-esteem
  • Flight of ideas or racing thoughts
  • Increased talkativeness or pressured speech
  • Increased goal-directed activities or psychomotor agitation
  • Impulsive behaviour (such as sexual impulsivity or spending sprees)

Describe when a manic episode is diagnosed.

If the above symptoms are present for minimum 1 week with notable functional impairment, a manic episode is diagnosed

Describe when Type I bipolar disorder is diagnosed.

  • If such symptoms (of a manic episode) are present for minimum 1 week with notable functional impairment, a manic episode is diagnosed, leading to a DSM-5 diagnosis of type I bipolar disorder
  • NOTE: A depressive episode is not required for the diagnosis of Type I bipolar

Describe when a hypomanic episode is diagnosed.

If such symptoms (of a manic episode) are present for at minimum 4 days, but without notable functional impairment, a hypomanic episode is diagnosed

Describe the difference between Type I bipolar disorder and hypomanic episode.

  • Type I bipolar disorder requires the presence of the relevant symptoms (of a manic episode) for a minimum of one week with functional impairment
  • A hypomanic episode requires the presence of the relevant symptoms (of a manic episode) for a minimum of four days without functional impairment

Describe when Type II bipolar disorder is diagnosed.

If not a single manic episode had occurred ever, but only hypomanic episodes are present, along with at least one major depressive episode, then the DSM-5 diagnosis of type II bipolar disorder is made

Describe the difference between a Type II bipolar disorder and a hypomanic episode.

  • Type II bipolar disorder requires the presence of only hypomanic episodes with at least one major depressive episode
  • A hypomanic episode requires the presence of the relevant symptoms (of a manic episode) for a minimum of four days without functional impairment

What is the major difference between a manic episode and a hypomanic episode?

  • Manic episodes require presence of symptoms for a minimum of 1 week with notable functional impairment
  • Hypomanic episodes require presence of symptoms for a minimum of 4 days without notable functional impairment

Describe the difference between Type I bipolar disorder and Type II bipolar disorder.

  • Type I bipolar disorders require the presence of a manic episode lasting longer than 1 week
  • Type II bipolar disorders require the presence of hypomanic episodes alongside at least one major depressive episode

Describe when unspecified bipolar disorder is a diagnosis.

  • If manic symptoms occur for less than 4 days, or if other specific thresholds are not met for manic or hypomanic episodes, then the DSM-5 diagnosis:
  • “ Unspecified Bipolar Disorder ”

Describe the significance of psychotic symptoms appearing in an individual with a suspected manic episode in DSM-5.

If psychotic features are present, then hypomania cannot be diagnosed (since such features involve notable impairment by definition)

Describe the significance of hospitalisation appearing in an individual with a suspected manic episode in DSM-5.

Similarly, if a patient is hospitalised, irrespective of duration of manic symptoms, a manic episode is diagnosed, not a hypomanic episode

Describe the significance of antidepressant drug aetiology in an individual with a suspected manic episode in DSM-5.

If manic or hypomanic episodes are caused by antidepressants, then the diagnosis of bipolar disorder is still made in DSM-5 — this is an important change from DSM-IV where antidepressant- related mania/hypomania was viewed as an exclusion factor

Recall what the most consistent type of clinical features used to diagnose bipolar and mild depressive disorders are.

  • Psychomotor changes
  • NOTE: Mood is variable in the phenomenology of these conditions

What is cyclothymia?

This is is a mental and behavioural disorder that involves numerous periods of symptoms of minor depression and periods of symptoms of slightly elevated mood

Recall the proportion of patients that relapse after recovering from a mood episode.

~50-60%

Describe the amplitude of mood swings in either direction seen in Type I bipolar disorder.

  • Manic - high
  • Depression - high

Describe the amplitude of mood swings in either direction seen in Type II bipolar disorder.

  • Manic - low
  • Depression - high

Describe the amplitude of mood swings in either direction seen in cyclothymia.

  • Manic - low
  • Depression - low

Describe what is meant by rapid cycling of bipolar presentation.

More than 4 cycles of mood swings per year

Bipolar I, Bipolar Ii And Cyclothymia.

Describe the type of episodes that patients with Type I bipolar often experience.

In Bipolar-I, the majority of first episodes are depressive: — 85% have a depressive as first episode — 10% a manic episode — 3-5% mixed episode

What is the significance of the first manic episode for a patient with bipolar disorder?

Most (90-100%) of patients will develop more episodes after their first manic episode

Describe the long-term symptom presentations that occur in patients with bipolar disorder.

  • Most of the time (53%), individuals with bipolar disorder are symptom-free
  • Symptoms are present 47% of the time, and 33% of the time these are depressive symptoms

Mania, Anxiety And Depression.

Describe three reasons why DSM-III in the 1980s believed bipolar and unipolar to be completely separate entities.

  • Bipolar illness had an early age of onset (mean age of about 19 years vs about the late 20s for unipolar depression)
  • Shorter depressive episodes (in average 3m or less in bipolar vs in average 6-12m in unipolar)
  • Recurrent course (more frequent episodes in bipolar than unipolar illness, with rapid cycling, defined as four or more episodes yearly happening in about 25% of bipolar illness cases, but in ‹1% of unipolar depression cases).
  • Genetic specificity (manic episodes were found in families of persons with manic episodes, but not in families of persons with unipolar depression).
  • Differential treatment (antidepressants for unipolar depression vs neuroleptics and lithium for mania)
  • NOTE: Unipolar = without any episodes of mania

Describe the newer evidence present to challenge those beliefs about unipolar and bipolar seen in DSM-III.

  • MDD is commonly diagnosed in children, far below the mean onset of the late 20s
  • Brief depressive episodes that occur multiple times yearly are diagnosed in patients with MDD commonly, whereas such course of illness should be rare if MDD was a different illness than bipolar disorder
  • Genetic studies have found high rates of depressive episodes, without mania, in persons with bipolar illness, and also frequent occurrence of bipolar illness in relatives of those with unipolar depression
  • Treatment now overlaps considerably, with neuroleptic agents proven effective not only for mania, but also for depression, both in bipolar and unipolar types
  • Lithium has been well known to be effective not only for mania, but also for depression, both in bipolar and unipolar types

Recall a drug that can be used to treat bipolar and unipolar disorders.

Lithium

Describe the difference in heritability between bipolar and major depressive disorder.

Bipolar is considered more heritable than major depressive disorder

Describe the difference in insight between depression and mania.

In general, insight is preserved in depression, and impaired in mania

What are monoamines?

Monoamine neurotransmitters are neurotransmitters and neuromodulators that contain one amino group connected to an aromatic ring by a two-carbon chain

Recall three monoamines.

  • Dopamine
  • Norepinephrine
  • Serotonin

What is the monoamine deficiency hypothesis?

The “monoamine deficiency hypothesis” of depression suggests that depressive symptoms arise from insufficient levels of monoamine neurotransmitters serotonin (or 5- hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT), norepinephrine, and/or dopamine

Describe five pieces of indirect evidence that support the monoamine deficiency hypothesis.

  • 5-HT depletion by the antihypertensive drug reserpine could cause depression
  • Clinically useful antidepressants all increase synaptic monoamine (some selectively 5-HT) concentrations
  • Post-mortem evidence of reduced 5-HT levels in brainstem of individuals who committed suicide ( Mann 2013 ).
  • Lower levels of 5-HT1A-receptors ( Bhagwagar 2004, Sargent 2000 ) and 5-HT4-receptors ( Madsen 2014 ).
  • Monoamine oxidase A increased in MDD ( Meyer 2006 ). — Monoamine oxidase A breaks down serotonin
  • Blockade of serotonin synthesis by the tryptophan hydroxylase inhibitor p-chlorophenylalanine prevents the antidepressant effects of both MAOIs and TCAs ( Shopsin 1975 & 1976 )
  • Tryptophan depletion (brain serotonin) triggers relapse in MDD successfully treated with SSRIs ( Delgado 1999 ) or cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) ( Smith 1997 ).
  • Monoamine depletion correlates with decreased mood both in at risk and MDD in remission ( Ruhe 2007 )
  • Depression-related traits; “pessimism” ( Meyer 2003 )and “dysfunctional attitudes” ( Bhagwagar 2006 ) in MDD, and traits “negativism” ( Soloff 2010 ) and “neuroticism” ( Frokjaer 2008 & 2010 ) in healthy, related to 5-HT2A-receptor upregulation — 5-HT2A-receptor upregulation is a compensatory mechanism for decreased serotonin levels

What is tryptophan?

Tryptophan is an essential amino acid, meaning your body can't produce it and you must get it from your diet

Describe what changes 5-HT2A receptor expression.

Increased 5-HT2A receptor expression is a compensatory mechanism for low serotonin levels
