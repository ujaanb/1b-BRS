Overview

Recall four types of treatment for people with mental illnesses.

  • Chemical
  • Electrical stimulation
  • Structural rearrangement
  • Talking (psycho) therapies

Recall an example of chemical treatment for mental illness.

  • Drugs for psychosis
  • Drugs for depression

Recall an example of electrical stimulation treatment for mental illness.

  • Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) for depression
  • Neurostimulation for pain syndromes

What is ECT?

  • Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) is a treatment method that involves passing a small electric current through the brain, which intentionally causes a brief seizure
  • The seizure is believed to trigger changes in brain chemistry that

can quickly improve symptoms of certain mental health conditions Recall an example of structural rearrangement treatment for mental illness.

  • Psychosurgery — neurosurgery that destroys certain brain tissues
  • Deep brain stimulation for severe depression — targeting electrical impulses to the brain

Recall an example of talking (psycho) therapies for mental illness.

Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) for phobias

Describe three ways we can classify psychiatric drugs.

  • Based on chemical structure
  • Based on what illnesses they treat — antidepressant - antipsychotic – anxiolytic – hypnotic
  • Based on their pharmacology

Describe a pro of classifying drugs based on their chemical structure.

  • Every drug has a unique structure, making it easy to allocate data regarding these drugs
  • NOTE: The WHO classifies drugs based on their chemical structure

Describe a con of classifying drugs based on their chemical structure.

Classifying drugs based on their chemical structure has no use in clinical decision making — i.e the chemical structure of the drug doesn’t inform us of its clinical effects

Describe a pro of classifying drugs based on what illnesses they treat.

Easy for doctors to choose a drug

Describe two cons of classifying drugs based on what illnesses they treat.

  • Many psychiatric medicines work in several disorders — antidepressants also treat anxiety and OCD (obsessive compulsive disorder) — some antipsychotics used as add on (augmentation) treatment for depression
  • Most psychiatric disorders have multiple symptoms and a single medicine might not treat them all — symptoms in depression include anxiety, insomnia, low mood, agitation, loss of pleasure, loss of appetite, poor concentration, loss of libido with likely different neurotransmitter mechanisms

Describe how drugs can be classified on the basis of their pharmacology.

For example, take drugs that target neurotransmitters — instead of antipsychotic – we can say dopamine blocker — instead of antidepressant – we can say serotonin (or with some drugs noradrenaline or dopamine) enhancer — instead of hypnotic or anxiolytic – we can say GABA enhancer

What is the GABA receptor?

GABA receptors are the receptors that are responsible for neuronal inhibition in the brain

Recall the two forms of GABA receptor.

  • GABA-A receptor
  • GABA-B receptor

Describe the difference between the GABA-A and GABA-B receptors.

GABA-A receptors are fast-acting, ion channel-linked receptors, but GABA-B receptors are slower-acting, G protein-coupled receptors

Describe how the GABA-A receptor works.

  • At the GABA-A receptor, GABA binds to the ion channel-linked receptor, and opens the Cl- ligand-gated ion channel
  • This allows for Cl- influx into the neuronal cell, causing hyperpolarisation and reducing neuronal excitability

Recall a drug that works at the GABA-A receptor.

  • Benzodiazepines
  • Barbiturates

Describe how the GABA-B receptor works.

  • At the GABA-B receptor, GABA binds to the G-protein coupled receptor, and causes an intracellular reaction
  • This allows for Cl- influx into the neuronal cell, causing hyperpolarisation and reducing neuronal excitability

Recall a drug that works at the GABA-B receptor.

  • GHB
  • Baclofen

What is Alprazolam?

  • Alprazolam is a GABA-A agonist drug
  • It is a part of the benzodiazepine family of drugs

Describe the mechanism of action for Alprazolam.

  • Alprazolam acts as a positive allosteric modulator at the GABA-A receptor
  • It hence enhances the effect of GABA at the GABA-A receptor by binding onto it

What is a positive allosteric modulator (PAM)?

A positive allosteric modulator (PAM) is a drug that binds to a receptor at a site different from the primary (orthosteric) binding site and enhances the effect of the receptor’s natural ligand without directly activating the receptor itself

Recall what Alprazolam is used to treat.

  • Generalised anxiety disorder
  • Panic disorder
  • Short-term treatment for anxiety
  • Alcohol withdrawal — to counteract neurological adaptations that occur following alcohol exposure

Describe the effects of Alprazolam on hosts.

  • Anxiolytic
  • Muscle relaxant
  • Anti-convulsant
  • Sleep-promoter

What is Baclofen?

Baclofen is a GABA-B agonist drug

Describe the mechanism of action for Baclofen.

Baclofen works by acting as a GABA-B receptor agonist

Recall what Baclofen is used to treat.

Spasticity

Describe the effects of Baclofen on hosts.

Decreases alcohol craving in alcohol dependent patients

What is an autoreceptor?

An autoreceptor is an inhibitory measure located on presynaptic neurones to prevent over-firing of action potentials

Describe the role of an autoreceptor in signal transmission.

During the firing of an action potential, neurotransmitters act on autoreceptors located on the presynaptic neurone to slow down the firing of presynaptic neurotransmitter

Neurotransmitters At Synapses.

Describe how enzyme-targeting drugs interact with enzymes.

Generally these drug treatments block enzyme activity

Recall two examples of drugs that target enzyme activity.

  • Monoamine oxidase inhibitors [MAOIs] for anxiety and depression
  • Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors for dementias
  • Lithium blocks glycogen synthase kinase for mood stability — glycogen synthase kinase is implicated in bipolar disorder

What is the name of a drug that blocks a receptor?

Antagonist

Recall three receptor antagonists.

  • Dopamine receptor blockers for schizophrenia
  • Serotonin receptor subtype antagonists for depression
  • Histamine receptor antagonists for sleep

What is the name of a drug that stimulates a receptor?

Agonist

Recall two receptor agonists.

  • Benzodiazepines enhance GABA → to induce sleep
  • Guanfacine enhance noradrenaline → to treat ADHD

Describe the general fate of neurotransmitters.

Most neurotransmitters are recovered and recycled via reuptake sites

Describe how psychiatric drugs interact with reuptake sites.

  • Many psychiatric drugs block these reuptake sites to increase neurotransmitter concentration in the synapse to enhance post- synaptic receptor activity
  • NOTE: Some drugs switch the reuptake site direction to enhance release — e.g. amfetamine for ADHD

Recall three neurotransmitter reuptake inhibitors and what they treat.

  • Citalopram – enhances serotonin (= serotonin reuptake inhibitor or SRI) - for depression and anxiety
  • Desipramine – noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor (NRI)= enhances noradrenaline - for depression
  • Methylphenidate – dopamine reuptake inhibitor (DRI)- enhances dopamine - for ADHD

Describe the role of the 5HT-transporter.

  • The 5HT-transporter transports serotonin from the synaptic cleft back into the presynaptic neuron
  • NOTE: 5-HT is the same as serotonin

Describe the difference between the 5HT-1A and 5HT-2A receptors.

  • 5HT-2A works to induce more excitatory effects such as psychedelic effects

What is a partial agonist?

A partial agonist is a drug that binds to a receptor but only partially activates it, producing a submaximal response compared to a full agonist

Describe the difference between a partial agonist and a full agonist.

A partial agonist has a lower max efficacy than a full agonist

Describe the advantages of partial agonists.

  • Improved safety – especially in overdose
  • In states of high neurotransmitter or excess agonist, the medicine

can act as an antagonist

Recall a partial agonist.

What is aripiprazole?

Aripiprazole is a partial dopamine receptor agonist used in the treatment of schizophrenia

Describe the clinical importance of aripiprazole.

  • Aripiprazole is a partial dopamine agonist used in the treatment of schizophrenia and other psychotic disorders
  • In these disorders, dopamine levels are often too high
  • To combat this, we may use dopamine antagonists like haloperidol to reduce symptoms, however they often cause extrapyramidal side effects (EPSEs) due to the complete blockage of dopamine in the nigrostriatal pathway
  • Aripiprazole can hence be helpful as it works as a partial agonist, reducing the effect that a ligand has at the dopamine receptor, and since it doesn’t completely block the receptor, it reduces the risk of extrapyramidal side effects (EPSEs)

Describe how partial agonists can behave like antagonists.

  • In the presence of a stronger agonist, partial agonists can behave like receptor antagonists
  • This is because of the competitive antagonism displayed by partial agonists in the presence of stronger agonists
  • By competing with the stronger agonists for the same receptors and inducing a weaker effect, these partial agonists induce an antagonist- like effect, by reducing the effect of the stronger agonist
  • NOTE: However, on their own, partial agonists behave like partial agonists

What is an inverse agonist?

In pharmacology, an inverse agonist is a drug that binds to the same receptor as an agonist but induces a pharmacological response opposite to that of the agonist

Describe the effect of an inverse agonist.

  • Inverse agonists have opposite effects to agonists
  • For example, an inverse GABA agonist leads to anti-alcohol effects as alcohol increases the potency of the GABA system

Describe the difference between an inverse agonist and an antagonist.

  • An antagonist prevents the receptor from being activated but does not affect its baseline activity
  • An inverse agonist actively reduces receptor activity below its natural baseline, inducing an opposite effect to an agonist

Describe what is meant by a receptor subtype.

  • A receptor subtype refers to a different variation or form of a specific receptor type, within a receptor family
  • It has distinct physiological functions and pharmacological characteristics to other receptors in its family
