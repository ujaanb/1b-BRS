Overview

What is psychosis?

  • Difficulty perceiving and interpreting reality — i.e. failure of reality testing
  • NOTE: Psychosis is a clinical syndrome that can be caused by many disorders - the focus in research is often Schizophrenia

Describe the prevalence of psychotic disorders across the lifecourse.

Psychotic disorders have a ~3.5% lifetime prevalence

Recall five psychotic disorders.

  • Schizoaffective Disorder
  • Post-Partum Psychosis
  • Depression with psychotic symptoms
  • Bipolar Type I disorder
  • Schizophrenia
  • Delusional disorders
  • Drug-Induced Psychosis

What is schizoaffective disorder?

Schizoaffective disorder is a mental disorder characterised by symptoms of both schizophrenia and a mood disorder, either bipolar disorder or depression

Recall the three groups of psychotic symptoms.

  • Positive symptoms
  • Negative symptoms
  • Disorganisation symptoms

Recall the two main groups of positive symptoms observed in psychosis.

  • Hallucinations
  • Delusions

What is a hallucination?

Perception in absence of a stimulus

Recall three different ways that hallucinations may present.

  • Auditory
  • Visual (consider organic cause)
  • Somatic/tactile/formication
  • Olfactory
  • Gustatory — taste-related

Recall the different forms of auditory hallucination.

  • 1st (thought echo), 2nd, 3rd person
  • Running commentary
  • Command hallucinations

What is formication?

Formication is a tactile hallucination where someone experiences the sensation of insects crawling on or under their skin, even though there are no actual insects present

What is a delusion?

A delusion is a fixed, false belief not in keeping with social/cultural norms

Recall a special characteristic of delusions.

Delusions often have a theme/flavour

Recall the five “flavours” of delusions.

  • Persecutory/Paranoid
  • Reference — false belief that neutral events occurring around an individual are directed towards them
  • Grandiosity
  • Religious
  • Pathological jealousy
  • Nihilistic/Guilt
  • Somatic
  • Erotomanic — false belief that someone is in love with them, often someone of a higher status
  • Passivity experiences

What is a passivity experience?

Passivity experiences involve the feeling that one's thoughts, actions, or emotions are controlled or influenced by an external agent, rather than originating from within oneself

Recall the three forms of passivity experience.

  • Thought broadcasting
  • Thought insertion
  • Thought withdrawal — belief that an outside force is removing your thoughts

What is thought broadcasting?

Thought broadcasting is the belief that everyone knows what you’re thinking

What is thought insertion?

Thought insertion is the belief that your thought content is not autonomous

What is thought withdrawal?

Thought withdrawal is the belief that your thoughts are being removed from your mind by an external force or agency

What are first-rank symptoms of psychosis?

  • First-rank symptoms of psychosis are specific, highly suggestive symptoms of psychosis
  • These include auditory hallucinations, thought disorders (withdrawal, insertion, broadcasting), and delusional perceptions

Recall the four main groups of negative symptoms observed in psychosis.

  • Alogia
  • Anhedonia/Asociality
  • Avolition/Apathy
  • Affective flattening

What is alogia?

  • Paucity/poverty of speech (little content)
  • Slow to respond to questioning

Describe the expression of anhedonia in psychotic patients.

  • Few close friends
  • Few hobbies/interests
  • Impaired social functioning

What is avolition?

Avolition is a lack of motivation or inability to initiate and persist in goal-directed behaviours

Describe the expression of avolition in psychotic patients.

  • Poor self-care
  • Lack of drive/persistence at work/education
  • Lack of motivation

What is affective flattening?

Affective flattening is a symptom characterised by a reduced or absent expression of emotion

Describe the expression of affective flattening in psychotic patients.

  • Unchanging facial expressions
  • Few expressive gestures
  • Poor eye contact
  • Lack of vocal intonations
  • Limited emotional range

Recall the two umbrella terms used to group disorganisation symptoms in psychotic patients.

  • Bizarre behaviour
  • Formal thought disorder

Describe what is meant by bizarre behaviour.

Bizarre behaviour refers to actions or conduct that are odd, strange, or unexpected, potentially suggesting a mental disorder or brain damage

Recall three examples of bizarre behaviour in psychotic patients.

  • Inappropriate social behaviour
  • Bizarre clothing/appearance
  • Aggression/agitation
  • Repetitive/stereotyped behaviours

What is formal thought disorder?

Formal thought disorder describes a lack of logical connection between thoughts

Recall the various expressions of formal thought disorder from least severe to most severe.

  • Circumstantial thought
  • Tangential thought
  • Flight of ideas
  • Derailment/loosening of association
  • Word salad

What is circumstantial thought?

Circumstantial thought is when a person includes excessive, unnecessary, and often irrelevant details when speaking, making it difficult to follow the main point, although the person eventually returns to the original topic

What is tangential thought?

Tangential thought is where someone relates excessive or irrelevant details, never reaching the essential point of a conversation or question

What is flight of ideas?

Flight of ideas is a rapid, continuous succession of superficially related thoughts and ideas, often expressed as hurried speech with frequent abrupt shifts in topic

What is derailment/loosening of association?

Derailment/loosening of association is a lack of connection between ideas where speech shifts between topics with only minimal or no relationship, often without the speaker realising the disconnect

What is word salad?

Word salad is a confused or unintelligible mixture of seemingly random words and phrases

Describe the nature of psychosis onset.

  • Can occur at any age
  • Peak incidence in adolescence/early 20s

Describe how psychosis onset differs between men and women.

The onset of psychosis peaks later in women

Describe the general prognosis of psychosis.

  • Often chronic & episodic
  • Variable in prognosis

Describe what the morbidity of psychosis is like.

  • Substantial, both from disorder itself and increased risk of common health problems — e.g. heart disease
  • Significant impact on education, employment and functioning

Describe what the mortality of psychosis is like.

15-20 life years lost — i.e the mortality gap (Correll et al 2022)

Describe what the mortality of schizophrenia is like.

  • High risk of suicide in Schizophrenia — 28% of excess mortality
  • NOTE: Schizophrenia is a mental health disorder that can include psychosis as a symptom, whereas psychosis is a symptom itself that describes an altered perception/interpretation of reality

Describe why psychosis is often misdiagnosed.

  • Psychosis is often preceded by ‘prodromal’ symptoms. Hence it is often misdiagnosed as depression
  • NOTE: Prodromal = preceding, early signs/symptoms

Describe the prodromal symptoms of psychosis.

Prodromal symptoms may appear 6-18 months before florid psychotic symptoms emerge. They may include: — Increasing isolation — Poor self-care — Social withdrawal — Declining academic performance

Recall a predisposing factor that increases one’s risk of developing a psychotic disorder.

People at high-risk of developing psychosis often have/had another mental disorder like affective disorders earlier in life

Describe the role of genetics in psychosis.

  • Schizophrenia is highly heritable — ~46% concordance in MZ twins
  • Highly polygenic - there are lots of genes of small effect, but the ones found so far account for ~20% of known genetic risk

Describe three environmental risk factors of psychosis.

  • Drug use, especially cannabis
  • Prenatal/birth complications
  • Maternal infections
  • Migrant status
  • Socioeconomic deprivation
  • Childhood trauma

Why is the “build” of a psychotic patient important?

  • This is because if a patient has become really skinny and has lost a lot of weight, then they become a risk factor
  • Similarly, other factors such as dressing can be important

What is the Mental State Examination (MSE)?

A mental state examination (MSE) is a structured assessment used to evaluate a patient's current mental state, focusing on their behavioural and cognitive functioning

Describe the purpose of the Mental State Examination.

  • The Mental State Examination (MSE) gives us a snapshot at a particular moment in time on what the mental state of a patient is like
  • NOTE: It is not used to gain insight into the medical history of the patient, but it is more focused on the present

How many components are there to the Mental State Examination (MSE)?

Nine

Recall the nine components of the Mental State Examination (MSE).

  • Appearance
  • Behaviour
  • Speech
  • Mood/Affect
  • Perceptual disturbances
  • Thought content
  • Thought form/process — i.e train of thought
  • Insight
  • Cognition

Describe what we are looking for on the appearance & behaviour component of the Mental State Examination (MSE).

  • Bizarre or inappropriate clothing
  • Psychomotor retardation/agitation
  • Abnormal movements (medication related)
  • Self-neglect
  • Self-harm injuries
  • Echophenomena
  • Stupor and mutism (catatonia)

What are echophenomena?

Echophenomena are automatic, stimulus-bound repetitions of actions or sounds without explicit awareness, potentially indicative of an underlying disorder

Describe two examples of echophenomenas.

  • Echopraxia — imitation of another’s actions
  • Echolalia — imitation/repetition of another’s words/phrases

What is stupor?

Stupor is a state of near-unconsciousness or insensibility

What is catatonia?

Catatonia is a state in which someone is awake but does not seem to respond to other people and their environment

Describe what we are looking for on the speech component of the Mental State Examination (MSE).

Rate, rhythm and volume of speech is assessed

Describe how mood is impacted in psychotic disorders.

  • Some affective disorders can cause psychosis (e.g. bipolar disorder, depression)
  • Depression is comorbid with schizophrenia in ~30% of cases (Li et al, 2020)
  • People at high-risk of psychosis often have another mental disorder
  • Lifetime risk of suicide is ~5% in schizophrenia

Describe what we are looking for on the thought content component of the Mental State Examination (MSE).

Delusions

What is meant by thought insertion?

  • The belief that one’s thoughts are not one’s own and are being heavily influenced by a third-party
  • “I do think things that aren’t me… they’re putting thoughts in my brain”

Describe what we are looking for on the thought form component of the Mental State Examination (MSE).

  • This is not about WHAT you’re thinking (thought content), but HOW you’re thinking it
  • NOTE: Thought disorder manifests as disordered speech

Describe what we are looking for on the perception component of the Mental State Examination (MSE).

Hallucinations

What is a tactile hallucination?

Tactile hallucinations are false perceptions of touch, where someone feels sensations like something is touching them or moving on their skin, even when there's no actual physical stimulus

What is an auditory hallucination?

Auditory hallucinations are the perception of sounds, like voices or noises, that aren't actually present

What is a pseudohallucination?

A pseudohallucination is a vivid, involuntary sensory experience perceived as a hallucination, but recognised by the individual as subjective and lacking objective reality

Describe what we are looking for on the cognition component of the Mental State Examination (MSE).

  • Cognitive function
  • This is typically assessed using a formal test like Addenbrooke’s Cognitive Examination (ACE) or the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA)

What is meant by dementia praecox?

  • Dementia praecox is “dementia of the young”
  • This used to be what schizophrenia was described as

Describe what we are looking for on the insight component of the Mental State Examination (MSE).

The awareness into your own mental state, symptoms and need for treatment

Why is insight important to consider when treating someone with psychosis?

  • Patients with poor insight may have problems with the following:
  • Concordance with treatment
  • Attendance at follow-up
  • Willingness to be admitted to hospital
  • Impact on ability to have capacity to consent to treatment

Describe why it may be tricky to diagnose psychotic disorders.

  • Heterogeneity within disorder categories and episodes of illness — i.e differences
  • Many people who have a first episode psychosis will not have another — patients are hence followed up for 3 years under Early Intervention in Psychosis Services

Describe the difference between psychosis and schizophrenia.

Schizophrenia is a mental health disorder that can include psychosis as a symptom, whereas psychosis is a symptom itself that describes an altered perception/interpretation of reality

What is meant by biopsychosocial management?

Biopsychosocial management is a patient-centred approach to healthcare that considers the interconnected nature of biological, psychological, and social factors in understanding and treating health and illness

Describe the three groups of treatments we can administer using biopsychosocial management of psychosis.

  • Pharmacological — i.e biological
  • Psychological
  • Social support

Describe the pharmacological treatment for psychosis.

  • Antipsychotic medications
  • NOTE: Pharmacological treatment is often mainstay of psychosis treatment

Describe two psychological treatments for psychosis.

  • CBT therapy
  • Newer therapies like avatar therapy

What is avatar therapy?

AVATAR therapy is a digital mental health treatment for psychosis that uses computer-generated avatars to represent distressing voices, aiming to help individuals take control and reduce distress by engaging in dialogues with these digital representations

Avatar Therapy.

Describe two social treatments for psychosis.

  • Supportive environments, structures and routines
  • Housing, benefits
  • Support with budgeting /employment The neurotransmitter system for which neurotransmitter is most implicated in the mechanism of antipsychotics?
  • Dopamine

Describe the biochemical cause of positive symptoms of psychosis.

  • Increased dopamine activity in mesolimbic dopamine system is implicated in causing positive symptoms of psychosis
  • There is evidence of this from imaging + drug models + post-mortem studies

Which drug actions on dopamine receptors would be most likely to improve psychotic symptoms?

  • Antagonist
  • NOTE: Most antipsychotics are dopamine antagonists. Newer agents (e.g. Aripiprazole) are partial agonists

What is the mesolimbic dopamine system?

  • The mesolimbic dopamine system is a key brain pathway, also known as the "reward pathway," involved in motivation, reward processing and reinforcement learning — It connects the ventral tegmental area (VTA) to the ventral striatum (including the nucleus accumbens)
  • NOTE: This is one of the many pathways in the brain that dopamine is involved in — for example, dopamine is also involved in the nigrostriatal pathway which is responsible for motor control and habit learning

When can dopamine agonists be used in the treatment of psychotic disorders?

  • Dopamine agonists are usually used to treat Parkinson’s disease
  • However, they can cause psychotic symptoms

Describe the major side effects of antipsychotics such as Risperidone.

Extrapyramidal Side Effects (EPSEs)

Describe the role of the extrapyramidal tracts in the brain.

Involuntary (automatic) movements for balance, posture and locomotion

Describe the role of dopamine in the extrapyramidal tracts in the brain.

  • Dopamine is involved in the nigrostriatal pathway of the extrapyramidal tracts of the brain
  • This pathway is key for regulating movement by balancing excitatory/inhibitory pathways in the basal ganglia to prevent excessive/uncoordinated movement

Describe what causes extrapyramidal symptoms within psychosis sufferers.

Extrapyramidal side effects are caused by a dopamine blockade in the nigrostriatal (extrapyramidal) dopamine system — parts of the brain that enable us to maintain posture and tone

Recall the manifestations of extrapyramidal symptoms.

  • Parkinsonism
  • Acute dystonic reactions
  • Tardive dyskinesia
  • Akathisia

What is an acute dystonic reaction?

  • An acute dystonic reaction is a sudden, distressing movement disorder characterised by involuntary muscle contractions
  • It often occurs after taking certain medications, particularly dopamine receptor antagonists like antipsychotics and antiemetics

What is tardive dyskinesia?

Tardive dyskinesia a neurological disorder characterised by involuntary movements of the face and jaw

What is akathisia?

Akathisia is defined as an inability to remain still

Recall five clinical signs of Parkinsonism.

  • Bradykinesia
  • Postural instability
  • Rigidity - characteristic ‘cog-wheeling’ — jerky, ratchet-like movements, resembling a cogwheel turning
  • Slow and shuffling gait — Festination (chasing centre of gravity) — Lack of arm swing in gait – early sign
  • ‘Pill-rolling’ tremor - slow (4-6Hz) movement of the thumb across the other fingers:

Describe the management of extrapyramidal side effects (EPSEs).

  • Counsel about risk
  • Use lowest therapeutic dose
  • Use atypical psychotics as first line
  • Change medication to a more movement sparing agent
