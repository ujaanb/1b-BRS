Overview

Describe the homeostatic function of the kidneys.

  • Electrolyte balance
  • Acid-base balance
  • Volume homestasis

Describe the effect of kidney failure on its homeostatic function.

  • Increased potassium
  • Decreased bicarbonate — HCO3- is normally reabsorbed by kidneys
  • Decreased pH
  • Increased phosphate
  • Salt and water imbalance

Describe the endocrine function of the kidneys.

  • Erythropoietin (EPO) production
  • 1-alpha-hydroxylase (vitamin D) — NOTE: 25 hydroxycholecalciferol → 1,25 dihydroxycholecalciferol (calcitriol) in the kidney. This is catalysed by 1-alpha-hydroxylase

Describe the effect of kidney failure on its endocrine function.

  • Decreased calcium
  • Anaemia
  • Increased parathyroid hormone (PTH) — to compensate for low vitamin D

Describe the excretory function of the kidneys.

  • Nitrogenous waste (urea)
  • Hormones
  • Peptides
  • “Middle sized molecules” (mol Wt 2-5000 Da)
  • Salt and water

Describe the effect of kidney failure on its excretory function.

  • Increased urea
  • Increased creatinine
  • Decreased insulin requirement — due to reduced excretion

Describe the metabolic function of the kidneys.

  • Gluconeogenesis — primary in the liver
  • Insulin clearance — insulin is a small peptide hormone, so it's freely filtered through the glomerulus in the kidney

Describe the effect of kidney failure on its metabolic function.

  • Decreased gluconeogenesis
  • Decreased insulin clearance

Describe a general major effect of kidney failure on the body.

Increased risk of cardiovascular disease

Describe how the nature of the clinical presentation of kidney failure may vary.

  • The rate of deterioration in kidney function determines the clinical presentation of kidney failure — Increased rate of deterioration → Faster clinical presentation
  • This is because a more insidious onset can have less obvious effects on an individual’s day-to-day function

What is Kussmaul respiration?

Kussmaul breathing is a deep, laboured, and often rapid breathing pattern that occurs as the body's response to metabolic acidosis

Describe the purpose of Kussmaul respiration.

  • Kussmaul respiration aims to increase CO2 expulsion by increasing respiratory rate
  • It does this as a response to states of metabolic acidosis

Describe how Kussmaul respiration can aid those with kidney failure.

  • Individuals with kidney failure may struggle to excrete H+ (waste) and reabsorb HCO3-, leading to metabolic acidosis
  • This can be combatted using Kussmaul respiration, which aims to reduce CO2 levels by increasing respiratory capacity
  • NOTE: CO2 + H2O → H2CO3 → HCO3- + H+ meaning that reducing CO2 levels reduces H2CO3 and hence H+ production

Kussmaul Respiration.

Describe the effects of kidney failure on salt and water balance.

Impaired salt and water balance can be both a cause and symptom of kidney failure

Describe how impaired salt and water balance can be a cause of kidney failure.

  • While salt and water loss may be seen in tubulointerstitial disorders, damage to the concentrating mechanism → hypovolemia may be the cause of AKI
  • Damage to the concentrating mechanism refers to impairments in the urine concentration of solutes such as Na+, which can in turn lead to hypovolaemia
  • Hypovolaemia can then lead to reduced renal perfusion → kidney failure

Describe how impaired salt and water balance can be a symptom of kidney failure.

Kidney failure tends to REDUCE secretion of salt and water leading to: — Hypertension — Oedema — Pulmonary oedema

Recall a drug that can induce kidney failure.

Lithium

What is hyponatraemia?

  • Hyponatremia occurs when the concentration of sodium in the blood is lower than normal
  • NOTE: Hyponatremia ≠ reduced total body sodium. Sodium levels must be interpreted in the context of hypo-, hyper- or euvolaemia

How does acidosis arise in kidney failure?

Acidosis may arise via a failure to secrete and excrete H+ which normally occurs in the proximal convoluted tubule

Recall two effects of acidosis on the body.

  • Anorexia
  • Muscle catabolism — muscle → amino acids (i.e glutamine) → HCO3- generation

Recall two causes of hyperkalaemia via kidney failure.

  • ↓ Distal tubule potassium secretion
  • Acidosis — i.e H+ displacement of intracellular K+

How does hyperkalaemia arise via acidosis in kidney failure?

  • H+ levels rise due to reduced excretion
  • This H+ displaces intracellular K+ → increased extracellular K+ → hyperkalaemia

Recall three symptoms of hyperkalaemia via kidney failure.

  • Cardiac arrhythmias
  • Neural and muscular activity
  • Vomiting K+ DISPLACEMENT IN ACIDOSIS.

Describe the changes observed on an ECG in patients with hyperkalaemia.

  • Peaked T waves — reduced resting membrane potential
  • P wave — broadens — reduced amplitude — disappears
  • QRS widening
  • Heart block
  • Asystole
  • Ventricular Tachycardia / Ventricular Fibrillation

Ecg Changes - Hyperkalaemia.

Recall the effect of kidney failure-induced reduced erythropoietin (EPO).

Anaemia

Recall the effect of kidney failure-induced reduced 1,25 - vitamin D levels.

  • Reduced intestinal calcium absorption
  • Hypocalcaemia
  • Hyperparathyroidism — compensatory

Effects Of Chronic Renal Failure On Metabolism.

Describe the major predictor of end-stage renal failure.

  • Major predictor of end stage renal failure is chronic kidney disease (CKD) — BUT
  • Major outcome for a patient with CKD is cardiovascular disease
  • i.e. a patient with CKD is more likely to die from cardiovascular disease than end stage renal failure

Describe the difference between end-stage renal failure and chronic kidney disease (CKD).

  • Over time in chronic kidney disease (CKD), kidney function becomes so compromised that dialysis or kidney transplant is required for survival. This is end-stage renal failure
  • At this stage, the kidneys have permanently lost most or all of their function

Describe the long-term management of kidney failure.

Dialysis

What is dialysis?

  • Dialysis is a treatment that removes waste and excess fluid from the blood when your kidneys are not functioning properly, acting as a temporary replacement for the kidney
  • NOTE: Dialysis type is chosen based on the needs of the patient.

Describe the difference between peritoneal dialysis and haemodialysis.

  • Dialysis is based on the principle of replacing the function of the glomerular capillaries, which physiologically act as a filter which selectively allows substances into the Bowman’s capsule
  • In peritoneal dialysis, this filter is replicated using the peritoneum,

which acts as a semi-permeable membrane

(see lecture diagram / notes)

which filters waste products, excess fluids, and electrolytes, and returns the blood to the body

NOTE: Dialysis type is chosen based on the needs of the patient.

How the system works A Tenckhoff catheter is placed permanently into the abdominal cavity. Then cycles of “fill → dwell → drain” occur: 1. Fill

  • 1.5–3 L of dialysate run into the peritoneal cavity. 2. Dwell
  • Dialysate remains for 4–8 hours.
  • Wastes diffuse in; water moves by osmosis. 3. Drain
  • Spent fluid (now containing waste + extra water) is drained out.
  • Peritoneal Dialysis uses the peritoneum, the thin membrane lining the abdomen, as a natural filter.
  • A sterile dialysis fluid (dialysate) is introduced into the peritoneal cavity (the space inside the abdomen) through a catheter inserted into the abdomen. — Dialysate is a special sterile fluid that is introduced into the peritoneal cavity during peritoneal dialysis. — It is carefully formulated to have a balanced concentration of electrolytes (such as sodium, potassium, calcium), and it may also contain glucose to help with fluid removal. — The dialysate acts as the medium through which diffusion and osmosis take place, allowing waste products and excess fluid from the blood to move into the dialysate.
  • The peritoneum acts as a semipermeable membrane, allowing waste products, excess fluids, and electrolytes to pass from the blood vessels in the peritoneal lining into the dialysate through diffusion and osmosis.
  • After a set dwell time (several hours), the dialysate is drained from the abdomen and replaced with fresh dialysate

How the system works 1. Vascular access (AV fistula, graft, or central venous line) allows blood to leave the body safely. 2. Blood enters the dialyser (artificial kidney), which contains thousands of tiny hollow fibres. 3. Blood flows inside the fibres; dialysate flows outside them. 4. Waste products diffuse across the membrane into dialysate. 5. Excess fluid is removed by ultrafiltration. 6. Cleaned blood returns to the patient.

Peritoneal Dialysis.

Recall the three ways that end-stage kidney failure can be managed.

  • Conservative treatment
  • Home therapy
  • In centre therapy
  • Transplantation

Describe the conservative long-term management of kidney failure.

  • Erythropoietin (EPO) injections to correct anaemia
  • Diuretics to correct salt water overload
  • Phosphate binders
  • 1,25 vitamin D supplements
  • Symptom management
  • NOTE: The aim is cardiovascular risk reduction. There is no evidence that dialysis prolongs life in patients over the age of 80

Describe the home long-term management of kidney failure.

  • Haemodialysis
  • Peritoneal dialysis/assisted programmes

Describe the in-centre long-term management of kidney failure.

Haemodialysis, 4 hours 3 times a week

Describe the aim of dialysis in the treatment of kidney failure.

Dialysis is the bridge to kidney transplantation — It is a temporary kidney replacement

What is the kidney failure risk equation (KFRE)?

  • The kidney failure risk equation (KFRE) is a validated risk prediction tool for kidney replacement therapy in the next two or five years for adults with STABLE chronic kidney disease (CKD) Stages 3A to 5
  • It gives us a percentage chance that a patient is going to reach end stage renal failure

When can the kidney failure risk equation (KFRE) not be used?

NOT to be used in patients with rapidly changing eGFR

Recall four components used within calculation in the kidney failure risk equation (KFRE).

  • Age in years
  • Sex
  • CKD-EPI eGFR
  • Urine albumin creatinine ratio (ACR)

Describe how the kidney failure risk equation (KFRE) is used.

  • Patient understanding of their CKD diagnosis especially in the context of multi-morbidity
  • Identification of high risk CKD patients: — targeted patient engagement/education — aggressive risk factor management — referral to secondary care

Describe the considerations we need to make for the veins of patients with chronic kidney disease.

  • An arteriovenous (AV) fistula is how patients are connected to a dialysis machine and is usually created using the cephalic vein and brachial artery
  • For this reason, clinicians are asked not to take blood or insert IV lines into veins in the cubital fossa or the cephalic vein, and instead to use veins in the back of the hand — NOTE: The arteriovenous connection in an AV fistula causes increased blood flow into the vein, which over time causes the vein to dilate and thicken. This results in a larger, stronger vein that is suitable for repeated needle insertions required for dialysis.

Describe the considerations made for transfusions for patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD).

  • Generally, we avoid transfusions in transplantable patients with kidney disease
  • Transfusions → sensitisation → transplant failure
  • NOTE: Anti-HLA antibodies are typically made after transfusion, which increases the risk of a rejection if a patient needs a transplant in future. This is why HLA matching is now attempted for recipients and donors. Transfusions are used only when absolutely necessary

Describe how urea is utilised when assessing glomerular filtration rate (GFR).

Poor indicator — Confounded by diet (increased by meat), catabolic state, GI bleeding (bacterial breakdown of blood in gut), drugs, liver function etc

Describe how creatinine is utilised when assessing glomerular filtration rate (GFR).

  • Affected by muscle mass, age, race, sex etc.
  • Need to look at the patient when interpreting the result. TREND is helpful.
